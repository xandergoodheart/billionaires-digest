JPMorgan profit rises but securities losses sting

The move

JPMorgan Chase's third-quarter net income rose 6% to $15.3 billion and revenue climbed 8% to $50.2 billion, according to the bank's own SEC filing. Diluted earnings per share were $5.57, up from $5.07 a year earlier. The bank booked $658 million in investment-securities losses during the quarter, and investment banking fees rose 13% from a year ago but fell 8% from the second quarter.

Why it matters

JPMorgan's results are a bellwether for corporate borrowing and trading activity, so a securities loss and softer sequential investment-banking fees raise questions about how long the current trading boom can keep accelerating.

The bear case

Net income, revenue and per-share earnings all rose from a year ago, and investment banking fees are still up double digits year over year, so the quarter looks solid by its own numbers.

What to watch

JPMorgan's updated outlook for about $107 billion in 2027 net interest income excluding markets.

Citigroup profit jumps 40% but shares still slide

The move

Citigroup's third-quarter net income rose 40% to $5.2 billion, or $2.85 a share, beating the roughly $2.62 analyst estimate, on revenue up 8% to $23.85 billion, the bank's earnings release showed. CEO Jane Fraser said the bank is "continuing to invest in growth" while preparing for an AI-driven future. Shares still fell about 5.6% that day.

Why it matters

A profit beat that still gets sold off signals investors are more focused on the pace of trading and credit costs ahead than on the quarter that just closed.

The bear case

A 40% profit jump and 15% growth in wealth management are hard to call bad news; some of the drop may reflect bank stocks giving back gains built up ahead of earnings.

What to watch

Whether Citigroup can keep beating its own 11-13% near-term return target next quarter.

LVMH's China slump drags on fashion division

The move

LVMH's revenue for the first nine months of 2026 reached 57.1 billion euros, up 2% organically but down 2% on a reported basis, the company's own release showed. Third-quarter organic growth slowed to 1% from 3% in the prior quarter. Fashion & Leather Goods, LVMH's largest division and home to Louis Vuitton and Dior, posted a 5% organic sales drop in the quarter to 8.25 billion euros, which the company attributed to slowing demand in China. Shares fell about 1.7% on Oct. 13, the day after the report.

Why it matters

Fashion & Leather Goods is LVMH's main profit engine, so a second straight quarter of China-driven weakness there raises doubts about how soon the luxury slowdown turns around.

The bear case

The U.S., Europe and Japan all grew well in the quarter, and LVMH says it remains confident in its full-year outlook despite what it calls an uncertain environment.

What to watch

LVMH's full-year 2026 results, due early 2027, for whether China demand stabilizes.

Hock TanLeadershipAI & techAVGOAI read · ▼ Warning

Broadcom CEO says capital, not chips, now limits AI

The move

Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said at a conference in Singapore on Oct. 12 that access to capital, not chip or memory supply, is becoming the main constraint on AI buildouts. He said some AI investors "have no clue what they are investing in" and are setting expectations so extreme that it is "almost like they are in a casino," 247wallst.com reported. Broadcom shares fell 1.88% to close at $354.73 the next trading day.

Why it matters

Tan runs one of the AI boom's biggest chip suppliers, so a warning from him about how AI infrastructure is being financed carries more weight than the usual outside skeptic's take.

The bear case

Tan also said compute demand looks likely to stay strong through 2030 and that chip and memory shortages are easing, which is a bullish read on the underlying business even as he's wary of its financing.

What to watch

Broadcom's next quarterly report for whether AI infrastructure orders are still growing.

Jio Platforms nears India's biggest-ever IPO

The move

Reliance's Jio Platforms filed its draft IPO prospectus with India's securities regulator SEBI on June 19, 2026 and received SEBI's approval on Aug. 28. The offering is reported at about 37,700 crore rupees (roughly $4 billion), entirely new shares, with proceeds going mainly to repay Jio debt, Angel One reported. Roadshows wrapped up in September and October, led by Akash Ambani, who called the listing "a great responsibility" and the Reliance group's first public share sale in nearly three decades, alongside his sister Isha Ambani.

Why it matters

A Jio listing of this size would be India's largest IPO on record and a major test of investor appetite for its telecom-and-digital business separate from parent Reliance Industries.

The bear case

The exact subscription dates, price band and listing date are still not formally confirmed by Jio or the exchanges, so the terms could still shift before launch.

What to watch

Jio's expected subscription window around Oct. 21-23 and a listing near Oct. 28.

Dangote Refinery's IPO window closes after profit surge

The move

The Dangote Petroleum Refinery's "people's IPO" subscription window ran from Sept. 14 to Oct. 13, offering about 4.1 billion ordinary shares at 525 naira each, AllAfrica reported. The refinery posted first-half 2026 after-tax profit of roughly $1.82 billion, reversing a $476 million loss for all of 2025, as average plant utilization rose to 83.6% from about 45% at the start of the year.

Why it matters

A successful listing would make Aliko Dangote's refinery a public company for the first time, after years of construction delays and losses.

The bear case

Allotment and listing aren't finalized yet, and some of the company's own revenue and fundraising figures didn't fully reconcile in reporting, so the final outcome of the offer is still unclear.

What to watch

The refinery's planned listing, expected in late November.

HCLTech profit rises 21% on record deal bookings

The move

HCLTech reported fiscal second-quarter net profit of 5,132 crore rupees, up 21% year over year, on revenue of 35,620 crore rupees, up 11.5%, for the quarter ended Sept. 30, 2026, the company said. New deal bookings rose 27% to about $3.26 billion, a first-half record, and the board declared a 12-rupee-a-share interim dividend. Shares rallied the next trading day.

Why it matters

Strong bookings suggest enterprise clients are still signing new IT and AI-services contracts even as some peers describe a more cautious spending environment.

The bear case

HCL's software unit revenue fell 11.2% in constant currency, and the company left its full-year revenue growth guidance unchanged at a modest 2.5%-4%, so the quarter wasn't a breakout across the whole business.

What to watch

Whether this quarter's record bookings convert into faster revenue growth later in the fiscal year.

Samsung guides to record profit on AI memory demand

The move

Samsung Electronics guided on Oct. 8 to third-quarter consolidated operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won, nearly nine times the 12.2 trillion won it posted a year earlier, on sales of about 195 trillion won, Hargreaves Lansdown reported. The preliminary, company-estimated figures are required under Korean disclosure rules and don't break results out by division; the detailed report with segment figures is due later this month.

Why it matters

The jump points to how much revenue memory-chip makers are now pulling in from AI data-center demand for high-bandwidth and other advanced memory.

The bear case

The guidance is preliminary with no division breakdown yet, and the reported sales figure actually came in below analysts' consensus forecast.

What to watch

Samsung's detailed Q3 report, expected later this month, for the semiconductor division's actual contribution.
