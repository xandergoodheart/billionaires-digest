AI debt jitters wipe billions off Oracle, Dell and SoftBank

A viral chart of CoreWeave's bond yields, plus hotter US inflation data, sent investors fleeing AI-infrastructure stocks this week — hitting Larry Ellison's Oracle, Michael Dell's company and Masayoshi Son's SoftBank in the same 48 hours.

Oracle sinks 8.8% as AI debt fears spread to neoclouds

The move

Oracle shares closed down 8.8% on Oct 14, 2026, after a chart showing yields on a batch of CoreWeave's bonds briefly topping 15% went viral on social media, rattling investors in AI-infrastructure borrowers. Oracle isn't a "neocloud" itself, but it has borrowed heavily to fund its AI data-center buildout, which is why investors grouped it with CoreWeave. CoreWeave's own stock fell more than 15% the same day.

Why it matters

Oracle has taken on tens of billions of dollars in debt for AI data centers tied in part to its OpenAI cloud contract, so any sign that AI borrowers are stretched raises questions about whether that spending pays off.

The bear case

Oracle's contracted backlog still supports its spending plans, and a bad day for a competitor's bonds doesn't by itself change Oracle's own balance sheet.

What to watch

Oracle's reported talks with Apollo and Goldman Sachs on financing for its AI data centers, which it wants completed this year.

Dell drops 11% on Barclays downgrade over AI doubts

The move

Dell Technologies shares fell 11.3% on Oct 14, 2026, far more than the S&P 500's 1.1% decline, after Barclays cut the stock to Equal Weight from Overweight. Barclays said it believes Dell's strong enterprise revenue reflects elevated memory-chip costs more than a real shift toward enterprise AI spending, and made similar downgrades to HPE and NetApp the same day.

Why it matters

Dell's stock had run up on hopes that businesses are buying AI-ready servers in volume; Barclays' note challenges that story and questions how much of the recent growth is actually AI-driven.

The bear case

Dell's revenue still grew, and one analyst's reframing of the cause doesn't change the order backlog the company has already booked.

What to watch

Dell's next earnings report for concrete data on enterprise AI order volume.

Skydance stock plunges 22% after warrant payout

The move

Skydance, the renamed Paramount Skydance, fell as much as 26% intraday before closing down 22.2% at $7.06 on Oct 14, 2026, after distributing about 470.7 million warrants to former Paramount Class B shareholders. Each warrant lets holders buy one Class B share for $12; full exercise could raise $5.7 billion for the company while diluting current shareholders. The warrant payout came about a week after Skydance closed its $110 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, a deal backstopped by the Lawrence J. Ellison Revocable Trust.

Why it matters

The sell-off suggests investors think the combined company overpaid: Skydance's resulting market value of under $40 billion is less than half what Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount were worth separately before the deal, according to Variety.

The bear case

Much of the drop is a one-time mechanical dilution tied to the merger's close, not necessarily a reassessment of the combined company's underlying business.

What to watch

The 12-day antitrust trial over the merger, scheduled for March 2, 2027.

Masayoshi SonOtherAI & techSFTBYAI read · ▼ Bearish

SoftBank sinks as Asia tech stocks sell off

The move

SoftBank Group shares fell sharply this week as part of a broader Asia tech sell-off that also hit SK Hynix, Samsung and, in the US, Nvidia, AMD and Dell. The drop followed hotter-than-expected US inflation data that revived fears the Federal Reserve will keep interest rates higher, raising the cost of financing AI infrastructure.

Why it matters

SoftBank is a major OpenAI investor with further multibillion-dollar payments due, so worries about AI-funding costs and higher rates hit it harder than most.

The bear case

SoftBank's core Arm and Vision Fund holdings haven't changed; a rate-driven, one-week move isn't the same as a shift in the value of what SoftBank actually owns.

What to watch

SoftBank's next scheduled payment toward its OpenAI commitment.

JPMorgan's Q3 profit rises, but EPS trails estimates

The move

JPMorgan Chase reported third-quarter net income of $15.3 billion, or $5.57 a share, on Oct 13, 2026, up from $14.4 billion, or $5.07 a share, a year earlier, according to its SEC filing. Revenue rose about 8% to roughly $50.2 billion. The bank cut share buybacks 84% from the prior quarter and raised its full-year net-interest-income outlook to about $106.2 billion from $105.5 billion.

Why it matters

Profit and revenue both grew, but EPS came in below Wall Street's roughly $5.85-$5.92 consensus — an unusual miss for the largest US bank even as it builds capital.

The bear case

A one-quarter miss against analyst estimates doesn't change the bank's rising net-interest-income outlook or its still-large profit.

What to watch

Whether the steep buyback cut signals the bank is holding capital for a deal — Dimon has said JPMorgan is "on the lookout" for one worth $10-20 billion.

Carlos Slim's firm sells $122M in US energy stocks

The move

Control Empresarial de Capitales, Carlos Slim's holding company, sold 6.24 million shares of Talos Energy for about $106.8 million and 170,000 shares of PBF Energy for about $15 million, in trades made Oct 8-12, 2026, according to SEC Form 4 filings signed Oct 13 by Marco Antonio Slim Domit. The firm still held about 32.4 million Talos shares and 13.3 million PBF Class A shares afterward.

Why it matters

The sales trim two of the Slim family's independent bets on US offshore drilling and refining, even though the remaining stakes are still large.

The bear case

Selling roughly 16% of the Talos stake and a small slice of PBF isn't an exit — the family remains among each company's largest shareholders.

What to watch

Whether further SEC filings show the selling continuing.

Blackstone sells $9.8M more of its Bumble stake

The move

Blackstone entities tied to Stephen Schwarzman sold 2.76 million Class A shares of Bumble on Oct 13, 2026 for about $9.8 million, at $3.54 a share, under a trading plan the firm adopted in November 2025, according to a Form 4 filing. Blackstone's remaining holdings across its various Bumble entities ranged from about 13,700 to 6.97 million shares after the sale.

Why it matters

The sale continues Blackstone's gradual reduction of its Bumble stake, a dating-app investment whose stock sits far below its IPO price.

The bear case

The sale was made under a trading plan set up nearly a year ago, not a fresh decision, and Blackstone still holds a sizeable position.

What to watch

Bumble's next quarterly results for signs of a turnaround.

The move

Tesla unveils 'flying' Roadster at SpaceX site

The move

Tesla unveiled its long-delayed next-generation Roadster on Oct 15, 2026, in a livestreamed event at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas, with Musk, design chief Franz von Holzhausen and engineering lead Lars Moravy presenting. Tesla said the car can go from 0 to 60 mph in under one second, and the demo showed the car hovering and maneuvering off the ground using a SpaceX-derived thruster package. Musk said beforehand that the result was not "safety optimized" and that success wasn't guaranteed. No price was announced; prior reservations were $50,000 against an earlier $200,000-$250,000 target price, and production isn't expected before 2028.

Why it matters

The Roadster has slipped for nearly a decade since its 2017 reveal, and a working, if limited, flying demonstration is meant to revive interest in Tesla's halo car.

The bear case

The flying feature isn't approved for public roads and faces FAA restrictions, and Musk has a long history of missed Roadster delivery promises.

What to watch

Whether Tesla sets a firmer production date or price at a future event.
