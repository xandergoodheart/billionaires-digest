Barclays sees possible Tesla-SpaceX merger by year-end

The move

Barclays analyst Dan Levy wrote in an October 14 note that Tesla and SpaceX could announce a combination before Tesla's annual meeting, which must be held by December 6. He estimated Tesla holders might get about 0.7 SpaceX shares per Tesla share, calling that his own estimate rather than a proposal. Neither company has announced, confirmed or filed anything about a deal. Tesla closed down 2.15% to $367.16 and SpaceX fell 2.80% to $152.16 the day the note came out.

Why it matters

A tie-up would combine Musk's two largest companies under one roof, raising governance questions for Tesla's public shareholders and testing how much of Tesla's valuation already prices in SpaceX-style ambitions.

The bear case

This is one bank's conjecture based on public comments, not a reported process: no bankers, board committee or active talks have been disclosed, and prediction markets gave only a 24% chance of an announcement by year-end.

What to watch

Tesla's 2026 annual meeting, which must be held by December 6.

Palantir drops 7.2% as AI-financing doubts spread

The move

Palantir shares fell 7.22% on October 16 to $186.03, part of a broader software-sector selloff. ServiceNow and Salesforce also dropped as investors questioned how the AI infrastructure buildout will be financed. The stock closed about 11% below the record high it set October 9.

Why it matters

Palantir has been one of the most richly valued AI stocks this year, so it's a bellwether for how investors are pricing the risk that heavy AI spending doesn't pay off on schedule.

The bear case

The drop tracks a sector-wide move, not a Palantir-specific setback, and Goldman Sachs upgraded the stock to Buy with a $230 target just days earlier.

What to watch

Palantir's third-quarter earnings, November 2.

Berkshire adds $330 million to its Bending Spoons stake

The move

Berkshire Hathaway agreed to buy 10 million new shares of Italian app maker Bending Spoons at $33 each, a $330 million private placement that raises its stake to about 4%. It follows Berkshire's $600 million investment at the company's July IPO. Bending Spoons shares jumped roughly 40% in early trading on the news. The company said the money adds financial flexibility for its acquisition strategy.

Why it matters

It shows Berkshire, now run day-to-day by Greg Abel, still backing an unconventional, fast-growing bet rather than only buying familiar blue chips.

The bear case

Bending Spoons is a small, single-company position built on a roll-up strategy of acquiring apps; a 40% one-day pop can also reverse quickly once the news cycle moves on.

What to watch

Whether Berkshire discloses any further increase in its Bending Spoons stake in a future 13F or filing.

Meta and Apple team up to run Muse AI model on Macs

The move

Meta said its open-weight Muse Spark Open model, with 816 billion total parameters and a 1 million-token context window, has been optimized to run locally on Apple's M5 Ultra Mac Studio using Apple's MLX framework. Two linked Mac Studios can hold the full model without cloud servers. Meta demoed it editing iOS app code and operating video-editing software locally. Zuckerberg credited Apple CEO John Ternus for the collaboration on X.

Why it matters

It signals a thaw between two companies that compete in AI and gives developers a way to run a very large open model without renting cloud GPU capacity.

The bear case

The hardware isn't cheap: a single qualifying Mac Studio starts near $7,800, and two are needed for the full model, so this helps well-funded developers more than everyday users.

What to watch

Whether Meta or Apple extend the arrangement to other hardware or future Muse model versions.

The move

Donations tracked so far total more than $330 million toward a project the administration has said needs about $400 million; Trump himself was not listed as a donor.

The bear case

LVMH's fashion and leather sales slide 5% as China weighs

The move

LVMH reported third-quarter revenue of €18.5 billion, up 1% organically, a slowdown from 3% growth in the second quarter. Its largest division, Fashion & Leather Goods, posted revenue of €8.2 billion, down 5% organically as weaker Chinese demand offset gains in the US. Analysts had expected a decline of only 2% to 2.5%. Watches & Jewelry grew 6% and Selective Retailing grew 8%, led by Sephora.

Why it matters

It confirms that a cautious Chinese consumer is hitting the world's largest luxury group harder than expected, adding to pressure on the broader luxury sector this year.

The bear case

US sales kept growing, and three of LVMH's other four divisions posted solid organic growth, so this isn't a group-wide collapse.

What to watch

LVMH's full-year 2026 results, expected late January 2027.

Interactive Brokers profit jumps, dividend nearly doubles

The move

Interactive Brokers reported third-quarter diluted earnings of $0.69 per share, up from $0.59 a year earlier, on GAAP net revenue of $1.91 billion, up from $1.66 billion. Commission revenue rose 12% to $602 million and net interest income climbed 19% to $1.15 billion. The company raised its quarterly dividend to $0.15 per share from $0.0875. Founder Thomas Peterffy also said in a CNBC interview that customers remain wary of autonomous AI trading tools even as the brokerage expands access to them.

Why it matters

It shows one of the largest retail and institutional brokerages still growing briskly even as rate cuts loom, with client caution about AI trading tools acting as a check on how fast that technology spreads through markets.

The bear case

Stock trading volume fell 24% from a year earlier, so some of the revenue strength is coming from interest income and fees rather than client trading activity.

What to watch

Whether AI-trading adoption among Interactive Brokers clients picks up in next quarter's results.

GM's Barra reaffirms 2028 eyes-off driving plan

The move

Mary Barra told CNBC's Jim Cramer on October 12 that GM remains on track to launch an 'eyes-off, hands-off' driving system in the electric Cadillac Escalade IQ in 2028, before expanding it to other models. She said she tested the system recently and found it improved since a March ride. The feature goes beyond GM's current Super Cruise, which lets drivers remove their hands from the wheel but still requires them to watch the road.

Why it matters

It's a reaffirmation, not a new announcement, but it signals GM isn't slipping on a timeline that competes directly with robotaxi efforts from Tesla and Waymo.

The bear case

GM already walked away from its Cruise robotaxi business, and a 2028 target for a Level 3 system that must work across all US highways leaves plenty of room for delay.

What to watch

Any update on the Escalade IQ's path to production as 2028 approaches.
