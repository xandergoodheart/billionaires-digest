Musk is a trillionaire again — this time on fundamentals

Elon MuskIPO / MarketsAerospaceTSLA · PrivateAI read · ▲ Bullish

Musk becomes a trillionaire again as SpaceX surges

The move

Elon Musk's net worth hit about $1 trillion on Oct. 5, up $30.6 billion from the prior session, as SpaceX shares rose more than 5% to around $167 and Tesla shares also gained after a strong delivery quarter. Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas kept an overweight rating on SpaceX with a $300 price target. Musk also confirmed early talks with TSMC about a dedicated chip plant, Terafab, to supply Tesla, SpaceX and xAI. Musk briefly passed $1 trillion in June before giving it back as both stocks slid.

Why it matters

It's Musk's second trip over $1 trillion this year, and this time the move is tied to a strong Tesla delivery quarter, a clean Starship orbital launch and bullish analyst calls rather than hype alone.

The bear case

Both stocks are concentrated bets on Musk's own projects; if Starship or Tesla deliveries disappoint next quarter, the trillion-dollar mark could evaporate again as it did in June.

What to watch

Whether the SpaceX-TSMC Terafab talks turn into a signed deal.

Larry EllisonInvestmentAI & techORCLAI read · ▼ Bearish

Ellison's fortune drops $200B as Oracle's debt piles up

The move

Oracle, which Larry Ellison co-founded and still owns about 40% of, has nearly doubled its long-term debt to more than $160 billion over two years to fund AI data centers, making it one of the largest borrowers in the US corporate bond market. Oracle's shares have fallen more than 50% over the past 12 months as heavy capital spending pushed free cash flow negative. Ellison's fortune has dropped by almost $200 billion since Oracle shares peaked a year ago, though he remains in the top 10 globally at around $192 billion.

Why it matters

Oracle's AI buildout is one of the most aggressive debt-funded bets in the industry, and Ellison's wealth is now a direct bet on whether that spending pays off.

The bear case

If AI demand or Oracle's cloud contracts fall short, the debt load leaves much less room for error than rivals funding data centers out of cash flow.

What to watch

Oracle's next earnings report for signs free cash flow is turning positive.

Warren BuffettInvestmentFinanceBRK.A · LENAI read · ▲ Bullish

Berkshire adds to Lennar stake as homebuilder slumps

The move

Berkshire Hathaway bought 2,361,556 Class A and 15,028 Class B shares of homebuilder Lennar on Oct. 1-2 for about $192.6 million, paying between $77.12 and $81.90 per share. The purchase brings Berkshire's holding to over 28.4 million Class A shares and 568,028 Class B shares, pushing its stake past the 10% ownership threshold that triggers insider filings. Lennar shares were trading near $74.44, close to a 52-week low, after falling nearly 40% over the past year.

Why it matters

Buffett is adding to housing bets while the sector is out of favor, continuing Berkshire's build-out of a homebuilding presence that also includes its Taylor Morrison acquisition this summer.

The bear case

Homebuilders are cyclical and tied to mortgage rates; a bigger stake doesn't guarantee a quick turnaround if rates stay high.

What to watch

Whether Berkshire keeps adding to Lennar in its next quarterly filing.

SoftBank's Son warns 'superintelligence' could turn dangerous

The move

At a technology and society forum in Kyoto on Oct. 4, SoftBank's Masayoshi Son warned that 'superintelligence' in the wrong hands could become 'super dangerous' and said nations must cooperate rather than compete on AI safety, saying 'we don't have any more luxury of having human fight against human.' The warning comes even as SoftBank remains one of AI's biggest financial backers, having committed about $65 billion to OpenAI.

What to watch

Whether Son's call for global AI cooperation leads to any concrete policy proposal.

Ambani's Jio Platforms sets Oct. 21 date for $3.8B IPO

The move

Jio Platforms, the telecom and digital arm of Mukesh Ambani's Reliance Industries, plans to launch its IPO on Oct. 21, targeting about $3.8 billion in what would be India's largest listing to date, surpassing Hyundai Motor India's $2.9 billion IPO in 2024. Shares are expected to begin trading Oct. 28. The offering covers up to 270 million shares, about 2.9% of Jio's post-issue share capital, with most proceeds earmarked to repay debt owed by Reliance Jio Infocomm.

Why it matters

A listing this size is a major test of investor appetite for India's telecom and digital sector, and it starts to put a public market price on one of Ambani's most valuable units.

The bear case

The IPO is mainly aimed at paying down Jio's own telecom debt rather than raising new growth capital, so a strong listing doesn't necessarily signal fresh expansion.

What to watch

Investor demand and pricing once the roadshow begins ahead of the Oct. 21 launch.

Michael DellIPO / MarketsAI & techDELLAI read · ▲ Bullish

Dell stock jumps on record $95B AI server backlog

The move

Dell shares rose about 4% on Oct. 2 after the company reported a record AI server order backlog of $95 billion. Dell had already raised its full-year revenue forecast on Sept. 1 to a midpoint of $192 billion, citing AI server demand, with management saying the main bottleneck is memory chip supply — DRAM and NAND. Rival Hewlett Packard Enterprise also jumped 8% the same day after a large AI systems order from cloud provider Vultr.

Why it matters

A $95 billion backlog shows AI infrastructure demand is still outrunning supply, which is good for Dell's hardware business even as chip shortages cap how fast it can ship.

The bear case

A backlog is a promise to ship, not booked revenue; memory shortages could delay deliveries and push some of that revenue into later years.

What to watch

Whether Dell converts the backlog into shipped revenue without further guidance cuts tied to component shortages.

Aliko DangotePolicyEnergyPrivateAI read · ▼ Cautious

Dangote's $16B Kenya refinery hit by new legal challenge

The move

A group of 133 Lamu residents filed a case in Kenya's High Court seeking to stop construction of Aliko Dangote's planned $16 billion, 700,000-barrel-per-day refinery in Lamu. The court restricted excavation and building on the disputed land pending a hearing set for Oct. 14. Protesters are seeking compensation for land taken for the project, while environmental groups want the project's impact assessment made public. Dangote dismissed the protests on Oct. 3 as the work of 'local marketers and international players' without naming anyone, even after he and Kenyan President William Ruto had already broken ground on the project.

Why it matters

This is the second legal hurdle for the Lamu refinery, and a court-ordered construction halt adds real delay risk to a project Dangote is targeting for completion by 2030.

The bear case

Dangote has pushed through legal and political resistance on past projects, including his Nigeria refinery, so this challenge may end up being a delay rather than a derailment.

What to watch

The Kenyan High Court hearing scheduled for Oct. 14.

Mark ZuckerbergPolicyAI & techMETA · TSLA · NVDAAI read · — Neutral

Zuckerberg, Musk, Huang sign voluntary AI safety accord

The move

Six AI and chip company leaders — Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Google's Sundar Pichai, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, OpenAI's Greg Brockman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Tesla/SpaceX/xAI's Elon Musk — signed a one-page White House 'Accord on Super Intelligence' on Sept. 29. It commits signers to four layers of safety controls for frontier AI models: internal technical monitoring, an internal oversight team, an independent external auditor and an independent board committee. The accord is not legally binding and sets no penalties for falling short; it grew out of talks between Zuckerberg and House Speaker Mike Johnson, with Huang helping draft the principles beforehand.

Why it matters

It's a rare moment of several rival AI leaders agreeing to the same safety framework, even informally, while governments are still deciding how much to regulate AI.

The bear case

With no enforcement mechanism, public disclosure requirement or penalty for noncompliance, the accord is a voluntary statement of intent rather than a binding commitment.

What to watch

Whether any signer publishes audit results, or whether Congress tries to turn the accord into binding law.

Ken GriffinInvestmentFinancePrivateAI read · — Neutral

Ken Griffin gives $3B to build Carnegie Mellon's Miami campus

The move

Citadel founder Ken Griffin pledged $3 billion to Carnegie Mellon University on Sept. 30, which the university called the largest single gift to a US university. About $2 billion of the gift will build a new 35-acre Carnegie Mellon campus in Miami's Wynwood neighborhood, expected to enroll roughly 3,500 undergraduate, master's and doctoral students, with construction set to start next year. Griffin is joining Carnegie Mellon's Board of Trustees.

Why it matters

The gift anchors a major university campus in Miami, extending Griffin's push to build up the city since moving Citadel's headquarters there from Chicago in 2022.

The bear case

A single donor funding a new campus ties its fate to one person's continued involvement and giving, rather than a broader, diversified funding base.

What to watch

Progress on construction once it breaks ground next year.

Slim's América Móvil closes Brazilian ISP deal

The move

América Móvil, controlled by Carlos Slim, closed its purchase of 72.83% of Brazilian internet provider Desktop on Oct. 1 through its Claro NXT Telecomunicações unit, acquiring 84.7 million shares for about 1.75 billion reals ($334.3 million), or roughly $3.95 per share. Desktop serves more than 1.2 million subscribers across over 200 Brazilian cities. América Móvil shares rose after the deal was reported.

Why it matters

It's a small but concrete expansion of Slim's Latin American telecom footprint into Brazilian broadband, a market América Móvil has been building out through its Claro brand.

The bear case

The deal is tiny next to América Móvil's roughly $49 billion in annual revenue, so it won't move the company's overall numbers much on its own.

What to watch

Whether América Móvil folds Desktop into Claro's broader Brazilian broadband push or keeps it as a standalone brand.
