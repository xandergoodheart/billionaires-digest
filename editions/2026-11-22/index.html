Huang agrees to face Congress as Nvidia's China ties draw new scrutiny

Jensen Huang will sit for closed-door House testimony Dec. 1, and California's attorney general has subpoenaed Nvidia, as AI and chip stocks suffered one of their worst days of the year.

Huang agrees to face Congress on Dec. 1 over China ties

The move

Jensen Huang agreed to a closed-door interview with three House committees on Dec. 1, the House Foreign Affairs Committee announced Nov. 17. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the same committees Dec. 2. Huang said he would be "honored" to return as CEO if Nvidia's board decides that is in the best interest of shareholders, employees and the company.

Why it matters

Lawmakers want to know whether Nvidia chips or confidential information reached China, tied to reports about Huang's personal ties that led to his resignation on Nov. 12.

The bear case

A closed-door interview isn't a finding of wrongdoing, and Huang has said he's willing to cooperate; the decision on his return rests with Nvidia's board, not Congress.

What to watch

Dec. 1 House testimony and Dec. 2 Teter testimony; a separate Senate Banking hearing has also been requested for Dec. 3.

California subpoenas Nvidia over China chip concerns

The move

California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office served Nvidia an investigative subpoena, Reuters reported Nov. 19. The office is examining whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information reached China, and whether the company misled investors and the public. Nvidia has denied any wrongdoing, and no charges have been filed.

Why it matters

The probe follows reports tied to Huang's abrupt exit and adds a state-level inquiry alongside the federal scrutiny Nvidia already faces over chip access to China.

The bear case

An investigative subpoena is an early fact-finding step, not a finding of wrongdoing, and both state and federal authorities are still assessing whether further action is warranted.

What to watch

Whether California's inquiry produces findings, and how it intersects with Huang's Dec. 1 congressional testimony.

AMD falls after short seller alleges China firmware ties

The move

Short seller Hunterbrook Capital said Nov. 19 that updated AMD firmware had repeatedly turned up in server updates from IEIT Systems, a Chinese server maker the U.S. blacklisted in 2025, and in nearly identical firmware at three IEIT-linked companies. AMD fell as much as 11% that day and extended losses into Nov. 20's broader chip sell-off. AMD did not answer Hunterbrook's questions before publication.

Why it matters

Hunterbrook, which disclosed a short position in AMD, says it can't show how the firmware reached IEIT or prove AMD broke export rules, but the questions echo the China-access scrutiny already hitting Nvidia.

The bear case

The allegations are unverified and come from a firm betting against the stock; no regulator has acted on the claims.

What to watch

Whether AMD issues a direct response or regulators follow up on Hunterbrook's findings.

Anthropic prices record $81.3B IPO at $1.5 trillion value

The move

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Nov. 18, above its marketed $70-$78 range, selling 980 million shares for $81.3 billion in the largest IPO at initial pricing on record. Shares began trading on Nasdaq under ticker ANT the next day, valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. TechCrunch reported that Sequoia and former Google CEO Eric Schmidt are among investors holding roughly 1% of the company combined.

Why it matters

The pricing hands a paper windfall to Anthropic's early backers, including Schmidt, even though the IPO landed in the middle of a broad AI-stock sell-off.

The bear case

The fully diluted valuation is well below the roughly $2 trillion some investors expected over the summer, and one market recap said ANT fell about 8% in its second trading session.

What to watch

How ANT trades once the post-IPO volatility settles.

Musk says Tesla, SpaceX would 'probably' be better together

The move

At the New York Times DealBook Summit on Nov. 18, Musk said a Tesla-SpaceX combination would "probably" be worthwhile, but said no merger agreement, exchange ratio or timetable exists. He said any deal would need Tesla shareholder approval, which "may or may not succeed." He also said he shouldn't receive Tesla stock awards just for a SpaceX merger, and that any award should depend on Tesla's own performance.

Why it matters

The comments add detail to merger speculation that began when the two companies formed special committees in October, without resolving whether a deal happens.

The bear case

Musk says he's a conflicted party and can't take part in the talks, and SpaceX said in October the committees may never agree on terms.

What to watch

Whether Tesla and SpaceX's special committees reach agreed terms, and any shareholder vote that would follow.

Novatek to reroute Russian LNG from Europe to China

The move

Novatek CEO Leonid Mikhelson told reporters in Shenzhen on Nov. 18 that the company has "fully developed plans" to redirect a large portion of its LNG volume from Europe to China. He said Novatek will keep supplying Europe "right up until 11:30 p.m. on December 31," ahead of the EU's planned ban on Russian LNG starting Jan. 1, 2027.

Why it matters

Mikhelson said he doesn't expect the EU ban to be postponed, telling reporters "political motives outweigh economic considerations," even though Europe's gas prices remain high enough to keep Russian LNG flowing there for now.

The bear case

China may not be able to absorb all the volume Europe currently takes, and the shift could pressure the prices Novatek receives for its gas.

What to watch

The Dec. 31, 11:30 p.m. deadline before the EU ban takes effect Jan. 1, 2027.

GM hires search firm to find outside CEO candidates

The move

The Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 17 that GM is working with an executive search firm to find possible outside successors to CEO Mary Barra, while also weighing internal candidates. GM declined to comment and has not named a successor or given a timetable. Barra, 64, has led GM since 2014.

Why it matters

Looking outside the company marks a notable shift for GM, which has promoted its last several CEOs from within, as the automaker navigates EV strategy and autonomous driving.

The bear case

A search doesn't mean Barra is leaving soon; some analysts call an outside search a routine governance step that simply keeps the board's options open.

What to watch

Whether GM names a timetable or candidate.

Tencent profit dips as AI spending jumps 225%

The move

Tencent reported Nov. 18 that third-quarter revenue rose 8% to 207.3 billion yuan (about $29 billion), while non-GAAP operating profit fell 6% to 68.1 billion yuan as capital spending on AI computing jumped 225% to 65.1 billion yuan. Free cash flow was negative 69.1 billion yuan. Chairman and CEO Ma Huateng said "recent significant achievements across models and applications have accelerated Tencent's evolution for the new AI era."

Why it matters

The results show Tencent trading near-term margin for AI infrastructure, a pattern also showing up at Western rivals this earnings season.

The bear case

Revenue missed analyst estimates, and negative free cash flow shows the AI buildout is being funded from cash reserves rather than profit.

What to watch

Whether Tencent's cloud revenue growth, up 35% last quarter, keeps offsetting rising AI capex.
