Musk escalates Starlink attack on Ambani in India

The move

On October 9, Elon Musk posted on X calling Mukesh Ambani India's real "boss" and accusing him of "monopolistic exploitation" for blocking Starlink's launch in the country. India's telecom minister, Jyotiraditya Scindia, said the country "does not allow a monopoly in any sector." India's communications ministry said Starlink and two other licensed satellite operators are at the same stage of security assessments and are not being singled out.

Why it matters

India is one of the biggest untapped markets for satellite internet, and the dispute shows how much friction remains before Starlink can compete there against Ambani's Reliance Jio.

The bear case

Musk's public attacks on a named rival look like pressure tactics rather than a regulatory complaint, and India's government has already rejected the monopoly claim.

What to watch

Whether Starlink receives final security clearance to launch commercial service in India.

Jio Platforms sets price band for giant India IPO

The move

Jio Platforms is reportedly planning to price its IPO at ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 per share, valuing the company at up to roughly $107 billion at the top of the range. The anchor book is set to open October 19, public subscription runs October 21-23, and shares are expected to begin trading October 28.

Why it matters

At that valuation, it would rank among India's largest-ever IPOs and is a major test of investor appetite for Reliance's digital arm.

The bear case

The price band and dates are still reported, not officially confirmed by the company, and could change before the listing.

What to watch

Official confirmation of the price band when the anchor book opens October 19.

Tesla drops 'Full Self-Driving' name across Europe

The move

Tesla has renamed its driver-assistance system "Tesla Assisted Driving" on European websites, including Germany, Spain and the Netherlands. The change follows an October 6 statement from Germany's transport ministry calling the old "Full Self-Driving" name misleading because the system doesn't take over the full driving task. Tesla offered the new name during talks with Germany in September. The US site still uses "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)."

Why it matters

In exchange, Germany says it will back a 10% speed-over-limit cap in the EU approval process, which could help Tesla get the system approved more widely across the bloc.

The bear case

Critics say the rename is cosmetic and doesn't change what the system actually does or how safe it is.

What to watch

The EU-wide approval vote, which has slipped to December at the earliest.

Nvidia in talks to buy or deepen stake in Reflection AI

The move

The Financial Times reported on October 10 that Nvidia is in early talks to either expand its existing $800 million investment in AI startup Reflection AI or acquire the company outright, possibly through an "acqui-hire" structure. Reflection AI, founded by former DeepMind researchers, was reportedly raising capital in April at a $25 billion pre-money valuation. A deal could happen within weeks, or the talks could collapse.

Why it matters

It shows Nvidia moving beyond chips into owning more of the AI software stack as it competes with rivals like China's DeepSeek.

The bear case

No price or structure has been agreed, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the report.

What to watch

Whether Nvidia and Reflection AI announce a final deal in the coming weeks.

Amazon in talks to buy AI startup Decart for $7B

The move

The Wall Street Journal reported on October 11 that Amazon is in talks to acquire Decart, an AI startup whose software helps developers shift workloads between different AI chips, in a deal that could be worth about $7 billion. No agreement has been signed. Bloomberg had separately reported that Anthropic explored buying Decart for roughly $6 billion earlier this year but dropped the idea.

Why it matters

It's another sign of large tech companies buying smaller AI infrastructure startups to add computing flexibility rather than building everything themselves.

The bear case

The talks are early and a prior suitor already walked away from a similar price, so the deal could still fall apart.

What to watch

Whether Amazon and Decart confirm a signed agreement.

Zuckerberg rejects calls to slow down on AI safety

The move

Zuckerberg said "nobody needs to tap the brakes" on AI development, arguing individual labs, including Meta, have enough incentive to build safely without an industry-wide pause. The comments, made in September and expanded on in October interviews, come as Meta's Muse AI agent passed 6.6 million downloads and 1.8 million daily active users by early October, and as Meta has said it plans to invest up to $145 billion in AI infrastructure in 2026.

Why it matters

Zuckerberg's stance puts Meta at odds with rival labs that have called for coordinated safety pauses as AI capabilities advance.

The bear case

A New York Times investigation reported that competitive pressure, not just safety review, shaped the timing of Meta's Muse launch, which undercuts the self-policing argument.

What to watch

Whether other AI labs continue pushing for coordinated safety measures Meta is resisting.

Google defends $15B India AI data center amid water fears

The move

Google is building a $15 billion, gigawatt-scale AI data center in Visakhapatnam, India, set to open in 2028 and billed as its biggest AI hub outside the US. Local residents and activists have protested over water supply (local reports put daily supply at about 410 million liters against roughly 480 million liters of demand) and added power demand. Google says it chose air cooling over water cooling in response and has pledged to replenish 120% of the freshwater it uses globally by 2030.

Why it matters

It highlights the tension between the AI buildout's enormous resource needs and pushback from communities hosting the infrastructure.

The bear case

Google's water pledge is a global, aggregate commitment, not a guarantee for Visakhapatnam specifically, and activists want a full moratorium on new data centers in the state pending further study.

What to watch

Whether the project proceeds on its 2028 timeline amid continued local opposition.

Goldman Sachs upgrades Palantir to buy

The move

Goldman Sachs upgraded Palantir from Neutral to Buy on October 8, setting a $230 price target, about 18% above the stock's prior close. Analyst Gabriela Borges cited demand for sovereign AI, bespoke government and enterprise applications, and Palantir's industry-focused strategy. Shares rose as much as 2.5% in premarket trading the next session, nearing a 10-month high.

Why it matters

The upgrade reflects growing Wall Street confidence in Palantir's AI-driven government and enterprise business heading into its next earnings report.

The bear case

Palantir already trades at a rich valuation relative to earnings, and one upgrade doesn't resolve long-standing questions about how much growth is already priced in.

What to watch

Palantir's next quarterly earnings report.
