Dell's record AI backlog headlines blowout quarter

The move

Dell reported fiscal third-quarter (ended Oct. 30) revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103% year over year, with AI-optimized server revenue of $22.0 billion, up 291%. Operating income rose 251% to $7.4 billion and diluted EPS rose 287% to $8.82. The company booked $60.2 billion of AI orders in the quarter, ending with a record $132.3 billion AI backlog, returned $4.9 billion to shareholders, and added $25.0 billion to its buyback authorization. Dell raised full-year revenue guidance to $202.0 billion from $192.0 billion and non-GAAP EPS guidance to $29.68 from $25.50.

Why it matters

The results show AI server demand still accelerating industry-wide, even as some rivals have flagged supply constraints this earnings season.

The bear case

Dell's own Q4 guidance implies revenue growth slowing to about 69% from Q3's 103%, and a backlog this concentrated in AI hardware orders can swing sharply if even one large customer delays.

What to watch

Dell's fiscal Q4 guidance of $56.3 billion revenue; the fiscal year ends Jan. 29, 2027.

Alibaba's AI Cloud growth accelerates to 53%

The move

Alibaba reported revenue of 271.5 billion yuan (about $38B) for the September quarter, up 10% year over year and above analyst estimates. AI Cloud and Compute Services revenue rose 53% to 61.0 billion yuan, faster than the prior quarter's 45% growth. Non-GAAP net income was 19.9 billion yuan, up 92% from 10.4 billion yuan a year earlier.

Why it matters

The acceleration in AI Cloud growth suggests Chinese cloud demand is still ramping even as Alibaba and its rivals pour money into AI infrastructure.

The bear case

Free cash flow turned negative as the AI buildout consumes cash, and some outlets reported adjusted per-share earnings missed consensus despite the headline beat.

What to watch

Whether AI Cloud's growth rate keeps accelerating next quarter as new chip export rules take effect.

William DingEarningsAI & techNTESAI read · ▼ Caution

NetEase revenue slips as it raises its dividend

The move

NetEase reported third-quarter net revenue of 27.9 billion yuan ($4.2 billion), down 1.8% year over year. Games and related services revenue fell 1.7% to 22.9 billion yuan, and Youdao revenue dropped 12.3%. Net income attributable to shareholders was 8.4 billion yuan, down from 8.62 billion a year earlier. The board raised its quarterly dividend to $0.117 per ordinary share ($0.585 per ADS), payable to holders of record as of Dec. 3.

Why it matters

The results show one of China's largest gaming companies still raising its payout to shareholders even as its core games business contracts.

The bear case

A second straight quarter of declining games revenue raises questions about whether new titles can offset the slowdown before Ananta's January 2027 global launch.

What to watch

Ananta's multiplatform global launch, currently scheduled for Jan. 15, 2027.

Colin HuangEarningsAI & techPDDAI read · — Mixed

PDD's profit grows even as revenue growth stalls

The move

PDD Holdings, the owner of Temu and Pinduoduo, reported third-quarter revenue of 108.3 billion yuan, roughly flat year over year. Transaction-services revenue fell 12% to 48.5 billion yuan, while online marketing revenue rose 12% to 59.8 billion yuan. Operating profit rose 25% to 31.2 billion yuan, with operating margin up 5.7 percentage points to 28.8%. Net income attributable to ordinary shareholders was 29.5 billion yuan, up slightly from 29.3 billion a year earlier, and the company held 455 billion yuan in cash and short-term investments, up 8% from year-end.

Why it matters

Operating profit grew even as overall revenue stalled, suggesting PDD is cutting costs and subsidies rather than competing purely on price.

The bear case

Flat overall revenue, after years of rapid growth, and a 12% drop in transaction-services revenue point to intensifying competition in Chinese e-commerce.

What to watch

Whether Temu's US tariff-related cost pressure shows up more clearly in fourth-quarter margins.

SpaceX stock drifts back near its June IPO price

The move

SpaceX shares closed at $133.35 on Monday, close to the company's $135 June IPO price, after falling as low as $127.79 on Nov. 20 - briefly dipping below the offer price. The stock had traded near $145 the prior week before a rally tied to Anthropic's Nov. 19 IPO faded into a broadly weak session, according to The Motley Fool. Anthropic's IPO filing disclosed a commitment to spend up to $120.5 billion through 2029 on computing capacity from SpaceX's AI computing arm.

Why it matters

A major AI customer committing over $100 billion to SpaceX's computing business is now sitting alongside a stock price near its offer level, a sign investor enthusiasm for the AI buildout has cooled since the June debut.

The bear case

A stock trading near its IPO price five months after a record debut raises questions about whether the offering was priced too aggressively, or whether sentiment toward AI infrastructure spending has genuinely shifted.

What to watch

SpaceX's 180-day post-IPO lockup, which the company's own disclosures put at Dec. 8, freeing insider shares for sale.

The move

Arista Networks co-founder Andreas von Bechtolsheim sold about 300,000 shares on Nov. 18, in 13 lots at prices between $183.60 and $195.17, for a total of about $56.1 million. The Form 4 was filed Nov. 20 and left him with 179,455,156 shares.

Tether leaders' fund holds 27.9% of a gold royalty firm

The move

A Nov. 23 SEC filing shows Tether Global Investments Fund, S.I.C.A.F., S.A. - whose reporting persons include Tether chairman Giancarlo Devasini, CEO Paolo Ardoino and director Jean-Louis van der Velde - beneficially owns 21,354,627 shares, or 27.9%, of Elemental Royalty Corp, a gold and silver streaming and royalty company listed on Nasdaq and the TSX Venture Exchange. The amendment discloses an internal transfer of 7,502,502 shares between two Tether-affiliated entities on Sept. 2, 2026; it does not report a change in the fund's own stake.

Why it matters

It shows Tether's leadership continuing to hold a large stake in hard assets like gold and silver royalties, alongside the company's bitcoin holdings.

The bear case

The filing reports only an internal transfer between Tether-affiliated entities, not new buying, so it may say little about the group's current investment appetite.

What to watch

Any further Schedule 13D amendments disclosing a change in the fund's Elemental Royalty stake.
