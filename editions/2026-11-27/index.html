Wall Street starts pricing AI's debt, not just its stock

S&P put Nvidia's credit rating on watch and Oracle's borrowing costs hit record highs the same day, as a two-year AI buildout collides with mounting leverage.

Signal feed

4 moves
Jensen HuangOtherAI & techNVDAAI read · ▼ Debt watch

S&P puts Nvidia's credit rating on watch

The move

S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's AA issuer and senior unsecured credit ratings on CreditWatch with negative implications on Nov. 25, and lowered its assessment of the company's management and governance to neutral from positive. S&P said "recent events undermine our view of NVIDIA's M&G framework." Nvidia shares fell 3.9% that day, closing at $149.33, down from $155.38 the prior session, after trading as low as about $141 intraday, as part of a broader AI-stock sell-off tied to hot inflation data and rising borrowing costs across AI-linked companies.

Why it matters

A credit-rating warning is a different kind of alarm than a stock drop: it signals that bond investors, not just equity traders, are starting to question the AI buildout's finances, right as Nvidia's board is still investigating a foreign-intelligence matter involving Jensen Huang.

The bear case

S&P only put the rating on watch, not on a confirmed downgrade path, and said any cut would likely be limited to one notch, to AA-; Nvidia's revenue and cash flow remain far stronger than most AI-infrastructure peers.

What to watch

S&P's follow-up decision on whether to actually downgrade Nvidia's rating, expected after further review.

Oracle shares near 52-week low on debt worries

The move

Oracle fell 5.1% Wednesday to close at $103.91, down from $109.50 the prior session, and traded as low as about $100 intraday, within sight of its 52-week low of $99.34 set earlier in the week. The decline came as credit-default-swap spreads on Oracle's debt hit record highs and Mizuho cut its global AI capital-spending forecasts for 2027 and 2028. Oracle carries more than $117 billion in long-term debt after a recent $20 billion capital raise; the company says its OpenAI data-center contract is fully funded.

Why it matters

Oracle has bet heavily on building AI data-center capacity funded by debt rather than cash flow, so rising borrowing costs hit it harder than most of its AI peers, and investors are now weighing how fast that leverage is growing against the contracted revenue backing it.

The bear case

Oracle says its largest AI contracts, including with OpenAI, are fully funded, and the company's backlog of signed cloud-infrastructure business remains large even if spending plans eventually get trimmed.

What to watch

Oracle's next quarterly earnings report, due in early December, for an update on cash flow and the pace of its AI data-center spending.

SoftBank slides as AI-trade jitters resurface

The move

SoftBank's US-listed shares (SFTBY) fell 6.5% Wednesday to $8.72, from $9.33 the prior session. Its Tokyo-listed stock fell as much as 16% intraday local time, erasing much of the gain from Monday's rally that followed Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO. Reports tied the move to renewed doubts about AI-sector valuations following Anthropic's Nasdaq debut and a broader sell-off in Asian chip stocks.

Why it matters

SoftBank is widely treated as a proxy for the AI trade because of its large OpenAI commitment and its stake in Arm Holdings, so swings in AI sentiment tend to hit its shares harder than most diversified conglomerates.

The bear case

The Nov. 24 rally SoftBank is giving back was itself a reaction to one company-specific event (Huang's reinstatement at Nvidia), so the swing may say more about short-term trading flows than about SoftBank's underlying AI bets.

What to watch

Whether SoftBank's Tokyo shares stabilize once the broader AI-stock sell-off that started Nov. 25 runs its course.

Musk expands SpaceX share gift to kids' accounts

The move

SpaceX said Wednesday it will give one Class A share to every active "Trump Account" - tax-advantaged investment accounts for American children, of which the White House says nearly 70 million have been opened since the program launched in July. Musk posted on X that he will personally match the gift and "will be donating another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year." SpaceX's contribution is set to transfer next month; Musk's personal shares can't move until his lockup expires in June 2027. SpaceX stock rose 7.47% in overnight trading on the Blue Ocean alternative trading system after the announcement, having closed 1.3% higher on Wednesday.

Why it matters

The pledge keeps SpaceX's name tied to a signature Trump administration savings program and adds to roughly $10 billion in prior SpaceX-share commitments Musk has made toward children's accounts, at a time when SpaceX's pre-IPO valuation is a frequent talking point for his net worth.

The bear case

Musk's personal shares remain locked up until mid-2027 and SpaceX hasn't disclosed a total share count for the pledge, so the near-term cost to him and the eventual value to any individual child's account are both unclear.

What to watch

Confirmation next month that SpaceX's one-share-per-account gift has actually transferred into Trump Accounts.
