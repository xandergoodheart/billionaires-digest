Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

The move

Nvidia's board said Nov. 12 that Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and a board member, effective immediately. The move follows a board investigation opened Nov. 8 after Huang disclosed a relationship with an executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom U.S. intelligence has linked to Beijing; the New York Times reported Nov. 11 that Huang had a separate relationship with an Nvidia vice president who reported to him. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO while keeping her CFO title, and former Synopsys CEO Harvey Jones became Nvidia's first-ever board chair. Shares fell 4.3% Thursday after swinging as much as 14% intraday, closing just above a $4 trillion market cap.

Why it matters

It's a sudden end for the executive most identified with the AI boom, days before Nvidia's next earnings report and a planned closed-door briefing for House committees on his conduct.

The bear case

No permanent successor has been named, and a rushed leadership change this close to earnings could unsettle customers mid-cycle in the AI buildout.

What to watch

Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter results Nov. 17 and the board's search for a permanent CEO.

Alphabet discloses new $548 million Qualcomm stake

The move

Alphabet's third-quarter 13F, filed Nov. 6 for holdings as of Sept. 30, showed a new position of about 2.98 million Qualcomm shares worth roughly $548 million. Its stake in SpaceX grew to about 555 million shares worth $83.7 billion, nearly 95% of the disclosed portfolio.

Why it matters

The Qualcomm stake is a rare new addition to Alphabet's public stock filing as AI infrastructure spending accelerates, and the SpaceX position shows how dominant that single holding remains in Alphabet's outside portfolio.

The bear case

A 13F only shows U.S.-listed stock positions at quarter-end; it doesn't explain why the Qualcomm shares were bought or whether the position has changed since.

What to watch

Alphabet's next 13F, due by mid-February, for whether the Qualcomm stake grows.

AMD jumps on Google Cloud deal, analyst upgrade

The move

AMD said Nov. 9 its fifth-generation EPYC server chips will host Google Cloud's new TPU 8tx system, the first cloud TPU built with hardware-level encryption for secure AI computing. The same day, Oppenheimer upgraded AMD to Outperform from Perform with a $600 price target, citing AMD's data-center growth. Shares rose about 12%.

Why it matters

The Google deal extends AMD's AI business beyond its own chips into the hardware that hosts a rival's cloud systems, and Oppenheimer's upgrade projects AMD's data-center revenue growing about 135% in 2027.

The bear case

AMD didn't disclose pricing, availability or independent performance benchmarks for the new Google system, and the stock's 12% jump already prices in much of that projected growth.

What to watch

AMD's next quarterly report for whether data-center revenue growth tracks Oppenheimer's forecast.
