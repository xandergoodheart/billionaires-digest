Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

The move

Nvidia's board unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and a director, effective Nov. 23, eleven days after he resigned amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly believed was linked to foreign intelligence. The board said it was satisfied with Huang's candor and had received input from senior U.S. government officials indicating reinstatement posed no national security risk. Shares closed up 31.48%, their best single day since 2003, and interim CEO Colette Kress returns to being CFO only.

Why it matters

The reversal shows how tightly Nvidia's value is tied to one person, and how fast a board can change course under investor and political pressure - Nvidia's board had said on its Nov. 17 earnings call that it did not intend to reinstate Huang.

The bear case

The board's own independent investigation into Huang isn't finished, and reversing a unanimous 'no reinstatement' stance within days raises governance questions a good stock day doesn't erase.

What to watch

Huang's Dec. 1 closed-door testimony before three House committees on Nvidia's China ties, now as reinstated CEO, and whether the board's ongoing investigation changes anything.

Walmart raises outlook after strong Q3 sales

The move

Walmart's fiscal third-quarter revenue rose 5.2% to $188.8 billion, above the $187.4 billion analysts expected, with adjusted EPS of $0.65 versus a $0.64 estimate. Global e-commerce sales rose 17% and U.S. e-commerce rose 23%. The company raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to $2.84-$2.89 from $2.80-$2.87, its third raise this year.

Why it matters

A third guidance raise this year shows Walmart's scale and e-commerce buildout are still paying off even as other retailers struggle with costs.

The bear case

Reported EPS fell 19.5% to $0.62 because of investment losses, and the sales outlook still trails what some analysts had hoped for.

What to watch

Walmart's fiscal fourth-quarter results, expected in February, will show whether the holiday season matches the raised guidance.

JPMorgan names Bregje de Best as next CFO

The move

JPMorgan Chase said Bregje de Best, currently CFO of its Commercial & Investment Bank, will succeed Jeremy Barnum as the firm's chief financial officer once the company reports first-quarter 2027 results. Barnum, CFO for six years, will become a vice chairman and stay on through early 2028, supporting Chairman and CEO Jamie Dimon and leading the January and April earnings calls and the February Investor Day first.

Why it matters

Dimon is lining up his bench years ahead of any CEO transition, extending the succession planning that began with naming two co-presidents last year.

The bear case

A new CFO still needs a replacement at the Commercial & Investment Bank, and JPMorgan hasn't said who runs that unit next.

What to watch

JPMorgan said it will name de Best's successor at the Commercial & Investment Bank 'early next year.'

Binance wins $6.5B order after October hack

The move

A court in the Abu Dhabi Global Market granted Binance a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order against the unidentified people behind an Oct. 29 hack that affected about $6.34 billion in assets. Binance had already said roughly $2.85 billion was frozen or intercepted. Binance's outside lawyers at Gibson Dunn called it one of the largest publicly known freezing orders ever made.

Why it matters

The size of the order shows exchanges increasingly lean on civil courts, not just blockchain tracing, to chase stolen crypto across borders.

The bear case

A freezing order is a legal ceiling, not recovered money - Binance hasn't disclosed how much of the $6.34 billion has actually come back.

What to watch

Whether firms ordered to keep assets frozen, including Circle and World Liberty Financial, release funds back to Binance.

Citi names Dell a preferred AI-hardware stock

The move

Citi named Dell Technologies one of its preferred IT hardware stocks, citing a reported $95 billion AI server backlog and rising enterprise AI infrastructure demand; Dell shares rose in premarket trading on the news. The call came a week before Dell's fiscal third-quarter earnings, due today, Nov. 24, where options pricing implied about a 10% move on the report.

Why it matters

Wall Street is betting Dell's AI server backlog keeps converting to revenue, even as some investors worry about memory-chip supply constraints.

The bear case

Dell shares had fallen roughly 25% since mid-October before this call, trailing the S&P 500 by more than 24 points - one upgrade doesn't erase that gap.

What to watch

Dell reports fiscal third-quarter earnings today, Nov. 24, after the close.

Musk says Tesla and SpaceX 'probably' better together

The move

Speaking by video at the New York Times' DealBook Summit on Nov. 18, Elon Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would 'probably' be better off combined, citing overlap in AI, robotics and chip design. He said no merger has been announced, that any deal would need shareholder votes, and that he should not receive Tesla's performance-based stock awards just because of a merger.

Musk keeping the idea alive in public, without committing to it, keeps investors guessing about Tesla's future structure and his own pay.

He also said a Tesla shareholder vote 'may or may not' approve a merger and gave no timeline - this remains talk, not a deal.

What to watch

Whether Tesla or SpaceX forms a formal deal committee or sets a shareholder vote date.

Nadella pitches Microsoft as full AI infrastructure provider

The move

At Microsoft's Ignite 2026 keynote in San Francisco on Nov. 17, CEO Satya Nadella positioned Microsoft as a complete infrastructure provider for enterprise AI, pairing models, enterprise data, developer tools, security and Azure. He called Copilot 'the new operating system for work' and the event introduced Copilot Home and Copilot Managed Runtime, both available immediately, plus Autopilot, an autonomous agent beginning rollout to Frontier customers.

Microsoft is trying to own every layer of enterprise AI at once, from infrastructure to the chatbot, rather than compete model by model.

The bear case

Announcing a platform at a keynote is easier than enterprises adopting it - several tools shown, like Copilot Code, are not yet generally available.

What to watch

Whether Copilot Code reaches general availability in 'the coming weeks,' as Microsoft said at Ignite.

Mary BarraLeadershipAutosGMAI read · — Succession watch

GM searches outside the company for Barra's successor

The move

The Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 17 that GM is working with an executive search firm on outside candidates to succeed CEO Mary Barra, while also weighing insiders, including chief product officer Sterling Anderson. GM declined to comment, named no successor and gave no timetable. Barra, 64, has led GM since 2014.

Why it matters

A board running an external search, not just grooming internal picks, suggests GM wants fresh options rather than a lock-step handoff.

The bear case

This rests on anonymous sources, and GM hasn't confirmed a search exists, let alone a timeline for Barra's departure.

What to watch

An SEC filing naming a successor would be the first hard confirmation; none has been filed yet.
