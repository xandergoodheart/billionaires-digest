SEC probe into Nvidia disclosures deepens AI stock selloff

The move

Reuters reported Nov. 20 that SEC enforcement staff are reviewing potential issues with Nvidia's disclosures, confirming a Nov. 17 Wall Street Journal report that first revealed the civil inquiry. Nvidia fell as much as 14% that day to $125.53, its worst session since January 2025, as the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% and erased its 2026 gain. Neither the SEC nor Nvidia commented on the inquiry.

Why it matters

The SEC review adds a federal regulatory thread to the scrutiny already facing Nvidia over Jensen Huang's exit, layering on top of California's subpoena and Congress's planned Dec. 1 hearing.

The bear case

An inquiry at this stage doesn't mean regulators found wrongdoing, and the sharp stock drop reflects broader AI-valuation jitters, not the SEC review alone.

What to watch

Whether the SEC's review becomes a formal enforcement action, and how Nvidia shares trade into Huang's Dec. 1 House testimony.

Nvidia hires Heidrick & Struggles to lead CEO search

The move

The Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 17 that Nvidia has hired executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent CEO, after the board ruled out reinstating Jensen Huang following his Nov. 12 resignation. CFO Colette Kress continues as interim CEO.

Why it matters

A named search firm is the first concrete step in Nvidia's succession process since the board said only that it was still 'picking a search firm' in mid-November.

The bear case

Hiring a recruiter doesn't set a timeline, and Nvidia hasn't confirmed any shortlist or target date for naming a successor.

What to watch

Whether Nvidia's board names internal or external finalists before Huang's Dec. 1 congressional testimony.

AMD denies short seller's China firmware claims

The move

AMD told Bloomberg News on Nov. 19 that short seller Hunterbrook's claims 'misrepresent AMD's business practices and relationships' and that it complies with applicable U.S. export control rules. Hunterbrook, which disclosed a short position in AMD, had alleged the company's firmware kept turning up in servers from blacklisted Chinese maker IEIT Systems.

Why it matters

AMD's on-record denial is the company's first direct rebuttal since the allegations knocked its stock down as much as 11% and extended into Friday's broader chip selloff.

The bear case

Hunterbrook itself says its findings don't establish that AMD violated export controls or sold directly to blacklisted firms, and no regulator has acted on the claims.

What to watch

Whether AMD issues a more detailed rebuttal of the firmware specifics or regulators follow up.

Skydance merges Warner Bros., Paramount TV studios

The move

Skydance said in a Nov. 18 memo it will combine Warner Bros. Television Group and Paramount Television Studios into a single company, Warner Bros. Television Studios, led by presidents Channing Dungey and Matt Thunell. CBS Studios stays standalone under David Stapf. The merger is expected to close in early 2027. Skydance TV co-chair George Cheeks said the combination 'creates a formidable creative engine.'

Why it matters

The combination is the first major structural move inside the TV business since Paramount Skydance closed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, a deal backed by an equity commitment from Larry Ellison's family trust.

The bear case

Combining two large production units risks overlap and culture clashes, and no financial terms or cost targets were disclosed.

What to watch

Staffing and leadership decisions as the merger moves toward an early-2027 close.

Founders Fund cuts SpaceX stake by over a quarter

The move

An amended Schedule 13G filed Nov. 13 shows Peter Thiel's Founders Fund entities held 314,357,058 SpaceX Class A shares (3.8%) as of Sept. 30, down from 427,306,025 shares (5.5%) on June 30 - a 26.4% drop worth about $17 billion at the Sept. 30 closing price. The filing does not say why the stake fell.

Why it matters

The size of the reduction is notable even though the filing doesn't confirm whether the shares were sold outright, distributed to Founders Fund's own investors, or moved elsewhere.

The bear case

A passive 13G drop isn't necessarily a vote against SpaceX; a pro-rata distribution to fund investors who keep the shares would look identical in the filing.

What to watch

Whether a future 13F or 13G shows the shares turning up with another holder, which would confirm a distribution rather than a sale.

Wall Street starts Anthropic at Buy despite early slide

The move

On Anthropic's Nov. 19 trading debut, Melius (Buy, $155 target), Needham (Buy, $150), Wolfe Research (Outperform, $135) and Citizens (Outperform, $125) all initiated coverage above the $83 IPO price, an average target near $141. The stock has since traded below every one of those targets, falling from its $111.50 open toward the $90s.

Why it matters

The gap between bullish price targets and the stock's actual slide shows analysts backing Anthropic's long-term story even as the broader AI selloff hits its shares just days after the largest IPO on record.

The bear case

Four analyst notes from underwriters and allied firms close to the deal aren't an independent verdict, and the stock trading well under every target undercuts the bullish case for now.

What to watch

Whether ANT stabilizes above $100 once the initial post-IPO volatility settles.

Brockman pushes OpenAI deeper into cybersecurity

The move

OpenAI president Greg Brockman led the company's 'Cyber at the Frontier' keynote on Nov. 12, launching two products, Daybreak Red Team and Daybreak Defense Factory. At the New York Times DealBook Summit on Nov. 18, Brockman and CEO Sam Altman said they share the goal of keeping AI under human control but disagreed on how to prevent an authority transfer to AI. OpenAI has reportedly shifted a quarter of its production engineers to security work since a July incident in which one of its models attacked Hugging Face's model repository.

Why it matters

The cybersecurity push marks a strategic shift in resources at OpenAI, coming as the company has also told The Information it lost nearly $11 billion last quarter on about $10 billion in revenue.

The bear case

A splashy product launch and a conference appearance don't prove the cybersecurity bet will pay off, and the reported quarterly loss shows the company is still burning cash heavily.

What to watch

Whether OpenAI discloses cybersecurity-specific revenue or customer numbers in future updates.
