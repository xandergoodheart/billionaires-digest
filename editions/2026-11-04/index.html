California's billionaire wealth tax trails after Brin-led opposition

The move

Proposition 40, a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of California's roughly 200 billionaires, trailed 44% to 56% in early returns Wednesday morning, according to the Associated Press. Google co-founder Sergey Brin led the opposition: he moved out of state and donated $102 million to Building a Better California, a committee he founded to fight the measure. Billionaire opponents and business interests spent $316.7 million in total. Two competing measures that would nullify Prop. 40 if either outpolls it were also ahead: Proposition 42, which would ban wealth taxes outright, led with about 55% approval, while Proposition 41 trailed with 52% of voters rejecting it. SEIU-UHW, the union behind Prop. 40, signaled it would try again in 2028.

Why it matters

The result shows how far billionaires are now willing to go, including personally relocating, to stop a wealth-tax experiment, and other states weighing similar measures will be watching whether California's defeat holds once votes are certified.

The bear case

Votes were still being counted Wednesday morning, and California's constitution says either Prop. 41 or Prop. 42 beating Prop. 40 would void it regardless of its own result, so the final outcome isn't locked in yet.

What to watch

California's certified final results on Propositions 40, 41 and 42.

Tesla and SpaceX shares fall as Democrats sweep Congress

The move

Tesla fell 4.9% and SpaceX fell 4.8% in premarket trading Wednesday after Democrats won control of the Senate and were on track to take the House in the U.S. midterm elections, CNBC reported. Investors focused on the Texas governor's race, where Democrat Gina Hinojosa held a lead over Republican Gov. Greg Abbott; Hinojosa has called for a moratorium on new data centers, a risk for Musk since Texas holds a large share of U.S. data-center capacity and he moved Tesla's and SpaceX's headquarters to the state. Freedom Capital Markets' Jay Woods said "this is Elon Musk-land now," pointing to Tesla, SpaceX and other relocated Texas companies as ones to watch. Horizon Investments' Scott Ladner said the AI trade's next few days hinge on whether Abbott wins.

Why it matters

The selloff shows how closely investors now tie Musk's companies, and the broader AI trade, to the outcome of a single state election and the chance of stricter rules on the data centers that power AI.

The bear case

The declines were premarket moves before Texas was fully called, so the reaction could reverse once the race is decided.

What to watch

The final call in the Texas governor's race between Abbott and Hinojosa.

SoftBank slumps as Democratic sweep stokes AI regulation fears

The move

SoftBank Group shares slumped as much as 8% in early Tokyo trading Wednesday as Japan's Nikkei 225 fell as much as 3.6% to its lowest level since early May, Euronews reported, after Democrats won control of the U.S. Senate and were on track to take the House. Investors feared a Democratic sweep of Congress could bring more regulation and restrictions on AI development, compounding existing doubts about when heavy AI spending will pay off. The yen also strengthened against the dollar, pressuring Japanese exporters.

Why it matters

SoftBank's huge bets on OpenAI and AI chips make it one of the companies most exposed anywhere to a shift in U.S. AI policy, so investors read the election results as a direct risk to Son's biggest wagers.

The bear case

The sharp early-session drop reflects a reaction to political odds, not a confirmed policy change, and shares pared some of the loss as the session went on.

What to watch

SoftBank's fiscal second-quarter results, due Nov. 10, and whether a divided Congress actually produces new AI or data-center rules.

Jensen HuangIPO / MarketsAI & techNVDAAI read · ▲ Chip stocks rebound

Nvidia's market value tops $5 trillion again as chip stocks rebound

The move

Nvidia rose 2.3% on Tuesday, Nov. 3, as U.S. midterm election voting got underway, pushing its market value back above $5 trillion after four straight losing sessions, Yahoo Finance and a KuCoin market report said. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index climbed, ending a three-day losing streak, with Micron up 2.6%. Analyst Jay Hatfield described the session as a rotation "from safe tech, like Apple, into riskier tech, like chipmakers," after Apple fell 1.9% on margin-cost warnings. Morgan Stanley said the election outcome helped remove a major source of market uncertainty.

Why it matters

The rebound shows how much Wall Street's AI trade had been held hostage to election-result uncertainty; once voting was underway, investors moved back into the same AI infrastructure stocks they had been selling for days on bubble fears.

The bear case

The next morning, once the Democratic sweep was confirmed, some of the same AI-linked stocks sold off again on fears of stricter data-center rules, showing how fast that confidence can reverse.

What to watch

Whether chip stocks hold their Election Day gains now that Democrats have won full control of Congress.

Lisa SuIPO / MarketsAI & techAMDAI read · ▲ Record quarter

AMD posts record revenue, raises outlook on AI chip demand

The move

AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, a record, up 45% year over year, with GAAP net income of $2.4 billion and diluted EPS of $1.46, both up 95%; non-GAAP EPS was $2.09. Data Center segment revenue hit a record $8.5 billion, up 97%, which AMD credited to EPYC processor sales and the launch of its Instinct MI450 Series GPUs. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion, implying roughly 66% year-over-year growth. CEO Lisa Su said, "We delivered a record quarter with revenue exceeding the high end of our guidance," and said she expects "growth to accelerate further in the fourth quarter." Shares, already up 3.65% in Tuesday's regular session, gained another 7.6% after hours once the results were released, a KuCoin market report said.

Why it matters

The results show AI chip demand for AMD's EPYC processors and its newly launched Instinct MI450 GPUs keeps climbing, a direct challenge to Nvidia's hold on data-center AI hardware.

The bear case

AMD stock has swung sharply on AI-spending worries all year, and the guided $17 billion fourth-quarter figure still has to be hit before investors can be sure the growth holds.

What to watch

Whether AMD's fourth-quarter report in early 2027 shows MI450 production scaling as promised.

Arista Networks posts fastest revenue growth since its IPO

The move

Arista Networks reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion, above its own $3.3 billion guidance and up 58.5% year over year, with non-GAAP EPS of $1.20 versus $0.77 a year earlier and a 64% non-GAAP gross margin. The company raised its full-year 2026 revenue forecast to $13.3 billion and gave an initial 2027 target of about $18.5 billion, above the roughly $16.4 billion Wall Street had modeled. CEO Jayshree Ullal called it "another momentous quarter with the highest year-over-year revenue growth since our IPO" and said it reflects the company's "increased relevance for the generative and agentic AI era." Shares jumped after hours, with JPMorgan raising its price target to $270 from $250. Arista co-founder and largest shareholder Andreas von Bechtolsheim, through the Bechtolsheim Family Trust, holds about 14.6% of the company.

Why it matters

The networking gear that connects AI data centers together is turning out to be as sought-after as the chips inside them, and Arista's growth rate shows that demand isn't slowing.

The bear case

Arista has disappointed investors on margin or guidance details in several recent quarters despite beating estimates, so a strong headline number hasn't always translated into a lasting stock gain.

What to watch

Whether Arista's initial 2027 revenue target of about $18.5 billion holds up as the year progresses.

Lyndal Stephens Greth & familyIPO / MarketsEnergyFANGAI read · ▼ Spending outlook raised

Diamondback shares fall even as profit and output both climbed

The move

Diamondback Energy reported third-quarter revenue of $5.4 billion and net income attributable to the company of $1.6 billion, with diluted EPS of $5.69. Oil production rose to about 525 thousand barrels per day. The company raised its full-year 2026 capital-spending guidance to $3.95 billion to $4.00 billion, up from about $3.9 billion, while also raising its base dividend 7.3% to $1.18 per share and repurchasing 1.3 million shares for about $251 million. Shares fell 5.8% the day of the report. Lyndal Stephens Greth and her family are Diamondback's largest shareholder, with a 23.2% stake received when Diamondback bought the family's Endeavor Energy Resources in 2024.

Why it matters

The drop shows investors punishing an oil producer for spending more even as profit and output both rose, a sign Wall Street wants cash returned to shareholders rather than reinvested in the ground while oil prices stay range-bound.

The bear case

The company still raised its dividend, kept buying back stock and raised production guidance, so the underlying growth story hasn't actually worsened.

What to watch

Whether Diamondback's fourth-quarter report shows the extra spending translating into higher 2027 production guidance.
