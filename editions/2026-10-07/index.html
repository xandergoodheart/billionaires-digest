Lede of the day

Nvidia nears $6 trillion after record $150B buyback

Nvidia's board approved the largest share-buyback increase in corporate history, and the stock's rebound has pushed its market value toward $6 trillion for the first time.

Nvidia adds $150B buyback, the biggest ever

The move

Nvidia's board authorized an additional $150 billion for share buybacks on Sept. 28, lifting its total remaining authorization to $235 billion through fiscal 2028. The company called it the largest single buyback-authorization increase in corporate history, topping Apple's $110 billion authorization from 2024; the stock closed up 1.68% at $228.86 that day. Shares then hit a record high on Oct. 2, gaining 2.9% to push Nvidia's market value to roughly $5.7 trillion — less than $300 billion away from becoming the first public company to reach $6 trillion, and a rebound of nearly 25% from its late-July low after a two-month selloff had erased more than $1 trillion in value.

Why it matters

A buyback this size signals Nvidia's board sees enough free cash flow to fund both its AI data-center build-out and shareholder returns at the same time, at a moment when rivals like Oracle are piling on debt to do the former alone.

The bear case

A giant buyback can also be read as a company running out of better uses for its cash, and Nvidia's valuation now leaves very little room for a slowdown in AI chip demand to go unpunished.

What to watch

Whether Nvidia actually becomes the first company to close above a $6 trillion market cap.

Google loses bid to limit $3.2B ad-tech damages claim

The move

A federal judge in New York, Kevin Castel, ruled on Sept. 30 that a class of roughly 5,000 publishers can pursue about $1.7 billion in damages from Google over its AdX ad exchange, with USA Today separately able to seek about $1 billion (before trebling) and Daily Mail General and Trust about $600 million. The judge rejected Google's challenge to the damage calculations and dismissed claims from publishers using different ad-buying tools. The case builds on a 2023 Justice Department lawsuit in which a Virginia judge already found Google illegally monopolized ad-tech markets.

Why it matters

This moves Google's ad-tech liability from a legal theory into dollar figures a jury could actually award, and antitrust lawyers say the ruling increases pressure on Google to settle rather than go to trial.

The bear case

Under U.S. antitrust law, damages can be tripled, so the publishers' claims could realistically be worth far more than the headline $3.2 billion if a jury sides with them.

What to watch

Whether Google moves toward a settlement with the publisher class before a trial date is set.

SoftBank completes final $10B tranche into OpenAI

The move

SoftBank completed the third and final $10 billion tranche of its $30 billion commitment to OpenAI's latest funding round on Oct. 1, deployed through SoftBank Vision Fund 2 in three installments of $10 billion each since April. SoftBank's cumulative investment in OpenAI now stands at $64.6 billion, for roughly a 13% stake. SoftBank funded part of the commitment with $11.1 billion raised last month in the largest high-yield corporate bond sale globally, and it canceled the remaining $10 billion of undrawn capacity on an earlier $40 billion bridge loan.

Why it matters

Son is finishing what he started rather than scaling back, even as he's publicly warned about AI safety risks this month — he's betting big on OpenAI specifically while urging caution on the technology generally.

The bear case

SoftBank is funding part of this with debt, and a stake in a company that doesn't trade publicly is much harder to value or exit than one of Son's listed holdings.

What to watch

Whether SoftBank discloses further follow-on commitments to OpenAI's next funding round.

Dangote refinery cuts off fuel importers over blending

The move

Dangote's refinery stopped selling petrol to Nigerian marketers and importers who bring in fuel from abroad, according to the company, which accused those buyers of blending its high-quality petrol with lower-grade imported fuel. A refinery official said: "We are not selling petrol to those who are importing, since they are trying to blend our high-quality products with their ultra-low-quality imported products." The refinery said the blending made it impossible to tell its own product apart from fuel handled by intermediaries, damaging its reputation. Affected importers disputed the quality claims and said the move was really aimed at squeezing out fuel imports altogether.

Why it matters

Dangote's refinery has become big enough to set its own distribution terms in Nigeria's fuel market, and cutting off a class of buyers is a direct test of how much pricing and quality power it now holds.

The bear case

If importers are right that this is really about eliminating competition rather than quality control, it could draw regulatory scrutiny in a market Dangote already dominates.

What to watch

Whether Nigerian regulators weigh in on the dispute between the refinery and fuel importers.

Liang WenfengInvestmentAI & techPrivateAI read · ▲ Bullish

DeepSeek raises $12B+ with Tencent and CATL backing 2027 IPO push

The move

DeepSeek is targeting at least $12 billion in new funding, with the round potentially reaching roughly $15 billion (about 100 billion yuan) after demand outstripped its original 50-billion-yuan target, Bloomberg and CNBC reported Oct. 6. Tencent (Ma Huateng) and CATL (Robin Zeng) have committed some of the largest amounts. The raise was expected to close this month. DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities to prepare for a possible listing on China's STAR Market as soon as 2027, and is building a data center in Inner Mongolia that will use at least 160,000 Huawei AI accelerators instead of Nvidia hardware.

Why it matters

This is one of the largest private AI funding rounds in China's history, and the Huawei chip commitment shows DeepSeek deliberately building outside Nvidia's ecosystem.

The bear case

A STAR Market listing is still a year or more away and depends on Chinese regulators, DeepSeek's model momentum holding up, and investor appetite for AI stocks not cooling in the meantime.

What to watch

Confirmation that the funding round has closed and at what final size and valuation.

Steve CohenInvestmentFinanceSSPAI read · ▲ Neutral

Cohen's Point72 discloses 5.2% stake in E.W. Scripps

The move

Point72 Asset Management, Point72 Capital Advisors and Steven Cohen filed a Schedule 13G with the SEC on Oct. 5 disclosing beneficial ownership of 4,192,722 Class A shares of E.W. Scripps, or 5.2% of that share class, as of Oct. 2. The filing said Point72 holds shared voting and dispositive power over the shares through its Point72 Associates fund, with Cohen and the Point72 entities owning no shares directly.

Why it matters

A 13G, rather than an activist 13D, signals Point72 is building a passive position rather than pushing for changes at the broadcaster.

The bear case

A passive stake can still turn active later, and a 5%+ position is large enough that any shift in Point72's intent would move Scripps' thinly traded stock.

What to watch

Whether Point72 amends the filing to a 13D or adds to the stake.
