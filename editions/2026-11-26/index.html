SoftBank's Son explored, then dropped, $1T SK Hynix bid

The move

SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son recently approached Wall Street banks about financing a roughly $1 trillion buyout of South Korean memory-chip maker SK Hynix, The Information reported Nov. 20. SK Hynix closed around 777.5 trillion won (about $577 billion) that Friday, so the figure implied roughly a 73% premium. A SoftBank spokesperson said the idea 'is no longer under active consideration.' SK Hynix said it 'knows nothing at all about the matter.'

Why it matters

SK Hynix is one of three main suppliers of the high-bandwidth memory chips AI accelerators need, alongside Samsung and Micron, fitting Son's push to control more of the AI hardware stack after SoftBank's Ampere and OpenAI bets.

The bear case

SoftBank had about 5.39 trillion yen (roughly $34.3 billion) in cash at the end of September, about 3% of the bid size, and Korean law requires government approval for any foreign takeover of SK Hynix, which a senior official said would be a tough sell politically.

What to watch

Whether SoftBank revisits SK Hynix or another large AI-hardware target once financing or politics look friendlier.

Chip stocks give back Monday's Jensen Huang rally

The move

Nvidia closed down 5.86% and AMD closed down 8.86% on Nov. 24, giving back part of Monday's rally that followed Nvidia's board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO. Intel fell 8.15%, Broadcom fell 3.98% and Micron fell 11.01% the same day. The Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ closed down 1.99% and the S&P 500 fund SPY fell 0.49%, while the Dow fund DIA rose 0.79%.

Why it matters

Chip stocks gave back part of Monday's sharp gains; UBS's Mark Haefele said the swings were not a signal that the 'structural AI investment story has broken.'

The bear case

A one-day pullback after Monday's rally is itself unremarkable, but Nvidia stock is still well below where it traded before Huang's Nov. 12 resignation, showing the leadership episode left a lasting dent.

What to watch

Wednesday's GDP and inflation reports, and whether chip stocks stabilize or keep giving back Monday's gains.

Peter Thiel buys $122 million more of Palantir stock

The move

Peter Thiel bought 850,100 Palantir Class A shares for about $122.3 million in three open-market purchases on Nov. 20, paying between $142.91 and $144.61 a share, according to a Form 4 filed Nov. 24. He held none of the shares directly before the purchases.

Why it matters

The buy is Thiel's first disclosed personal purchase of Palantir stock in recent filings; it comes after he sold nearly 2 million shares under a 10b5-1 trading plan in March.

The bear case

The $122 million is a small fraction of Thiel's overall Palantir exposure, most of which sits in entities like Rivendell 7 LLC and PLTR Holdings LLC, so the buy may say less about his conviction than the dollar figure suggests.

What to watch

Further Form 4 filings showing whether Thiel keeps buying or returns to selling under a new 10b5-1 plan.

Blackstone-backed Medline sells $2.1B more stock

The move

Medline priced a secondary offering of 60 million Class A shares at $35.50 each on Nov. 17, worth about $2.1 billion, sold entirely by stockholders tied to Blackstone, Carlyle, Hellman & Friedman, GIC and an Abu Dhabi Investment Authority subsidiary; Medline itself gets none of the proceeds. The sale was set to close Nov. 23. A Form 4 for Stephen Schwarzman, filed Nov. 19, shows Blackstone-linked entities converting about 7.57 million Medline Class B shares into Class A shares on Nov. 17-18 to enable the sale.

Why it matters

It's the second time this year Medline's private-equity sponsors have sold down their stake, following the company's record $6.26 billion IPO in December 2025, as the backers use strong markets to cash out of their 2021 buyout.

The bear case

Repeated large secondary sales by the same sponsors can weigh on a stock even when the underlying business is healthy, since they signal insiders are steadily reducing their exposure.

What to watch

Whether Blackstone, Carlyle and Hellman & Friedman sell down further once any post-offering lockup lifts.

Tether leaders add $5.7 million more to Gold.com stake

The move

Tether International, S.A. de C.V. - controlled by Tether Global Investments Fund and, ultimately, Giancarlo Devasini, Jean-Louis van der Velde and Paolo Ardoino - bought 150,000 more Gold.com shares for about $5.75 million at $38.31 each on Nov. 23, per Forms 4 filed Nov. 25. That brings its direct Gold.com stake to 650,000 shares, separate from 3,370,787 shares the group holds through another Tether entity, TPM, S.A. de C.V.

Why it matters

Tether has been steadily adding to Gold.com, a precious-metals dealer, since a roughly $150 million PIPE deal in February, part of its broader push into gold-backed and real-world assets alongside its USDT stablecoin business.

The bear case

At $5.75 million, the purchase is tiny next to Tether's existing Gold.com position, so it may be routine portfolio management rather than a fresh strategic signal.

What to watch

Further Form 4 filings from Tether entities in Gold.com and its other public holdings.

Schwarzman buys $10 million more of a Blackstone fund

The move

Stephen Schwarzman bought 388,224 shares of Blackstone Private Real Estate Credit & Income Fund for about $10.2 million at $26.16 each on Nov. 20, split between two purchases, according to a Form 4 filed Nov. 23.

Why it matters

The purchase adds to Schwarzman's personal stake in one of Blackstone's retail-facing private credit vehicles, which invests in real estate debt rather than equity.

The bear case

As Blackstone's chairman and CEO, Schwarzman has an obvious incentive to support demand for the firm's own funds, so a personal purchase is not necessarily a read on the broader real estate credit market.

What to watch

Flows into Blackstone's non-traded real estate credit funds, a business the firm has been expanding to individual investors.

Berkshire raises ¥182 billion in new yen bonds

The move

Berkshire Hathaway issued five series of yen-denominated senior notes totaling ¥182.3 billion (roughly $1.2 billion) on Nov. 19, ranging from notes due 2029 at 2.624% to notes due 2056 at 4.571%, according to an 8-K filing. Mizuho Securities USA and Merrill Lynch International underwrote the deal under Berkshire's existing shelf registration.

Why it matters

Berkshire regularly taps the yen bond market, where its borrowing costs are low, to fund acquisitions and operations without touching its large cash pile.

The bear case

More debt, even cheap debt, adds to Berkshire's balance sheet at a time its insurance earnings have already been falling, including at GEICO.

What to watch

How Berkshire deploys the proceeds, and whether it returns to the yen market again given its history of regular issuance there.
