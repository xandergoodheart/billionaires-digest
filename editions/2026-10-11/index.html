Trump gives Musk, Brin, Huang science medals at White House

The move

President Trump awarded the National Medal of Science to Elon Musk, Sergey Brin, Jensen Huang and AMD's Lisa Su at a Washington ceremony on Thursday, October 8. Michael Dell and Microsoft's Satya Nadella received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation instead. Trump called Musk "our modern-day Thomas Edison" and said, "Because of these people here, we will always be on top. No one else can compete with us." Hours before the ceremony, the administration suspended Microsoft and several other companies from the green-card sponsorship program.

Why it matters

The honors double as a message that the administration wants to keep its biggest AI and chip players close, even as it tightens the visa pipeline many of the same companies rely on for talent.

The bear case

A medal ceremony changes nothing about earnings, chip supply or visa policy; it's a photo opportunity timed to distract from the visa suspension announced the same morning.

What to watch

Whether the Permanent Labor Certification suspension affecting Microsoft and Adobe expands to other sponsors.

US suspends Microsoft, Adobe from green-card visa program

The move

The Trump administration suspended Microsoft, Adobe and several other companies from the H-1B permanent labor certification (green-card sponsorship) process, citing fraud. Vice President JD Vance announced it on Thursday, October 8, saying the program "has become rife with fraud," and said he'd support eliminating the H-1B program altogether. Microsoft said it "look[s] forward to providing the Administration with additional information" and that it files H-1B petitions only for workers who meet the visa category's standards.

Why it matters

Microsoft is Steve Ballmer's largest asset and a major part of Bill Gates's fortune; a prolonged freeze on skilled-worker sponsorship touches the hiring pipeline behind their stakes.

The bear case

This hits sponsorship paperwork, not current staff or product roadmaps, and Microsoft says it's cooperating; the practical effect on the company may be small.

What to watch

Whether the suspension is lifted after Microsoft's response, or whether more sponsors are added to the list.

Goldman Sachs upgrades Palantir to Buy on sovereign AI demand

The move

Goldman Sachs analyst Gabriela Borges upgraded Palantir to Buy from Neutral on Thursday, October 8, with a 12-month price target of $230, implying about 18% upside from Wednesday's close of $194.12. The call cites demand for sovereign AI, bespoke AI systems and Palantir's forward-deployed engineering model. Shares rose 1.8% to $197.53 Thursday, outperforming a falling market (the S&P 500 fell 0.47%, the Nasdaq fell 1.25%).

Why it matters

Palantir is chairman and major shareholder Peter Thiel's biggest publicly traded position; a bullish turn from a major bank adds to the stock's case after a lagging year against the Nasdaq 100.

The bear case

Palantir still trades at a rich multiple on revenue growth that has to keep accelerating; one upgrade doesn't resolve the valuation debate, and Zacks Research cut its own rating to Hold around the same time.

What to watch

Palantir's next quarterly report for whether sovereign and government deals keep growing as fast as Borges expects.

Tesla's Shanghai factory posts its best month of 2026

The move

Tesla's Shanghai factory wholesaled 95,366 Model 3 and Model Y vehicles in September, per CPCA data reported around October 8-9 — up 5% from 90,812 a year earlier and 10.7% from August, its best month of 2026. Exports rose 58.3% year over year to 30,529 vehicles while domestic deliveries fell 9.4% to roughly 64,800. Shanghai wholesaled 743,060 vehicles over the first nine months of 2026, up about 22.5% from the same period in 2025.

Why it matters

Exports, not Chinese demand, drove the gain; Tesla has extended Model 3 and Model Y discounts in China through October 31 to keep domestic buyers coming.

The bear case

Wholesale figures count cars leaving the factory, not retail deliveries, and domestic China demand is still falling even as exports mask it.

What to watch

Whether October discounts revive domestic Chinese demand, and Tesla's Q3 earnings report.

Elon MuskPolicyAutosTSLAAI read · — Name over substance

Tesla renames 'Full Self-Driving' to 'Assisted Driving' in Europe

The move

Tesla began calling its "Full Self-Driving" feature "Tesla Assisted Driving" on its European websites, Reuters reported October 9. Pages in Slovakia, Germany, Spain and the Netherlands now redirect to the new name, after Germany's transport ministry said the old name was misleading since the system requires constant driver supervision. The US site still uses "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)." Sweden and Italy have also raised concerns about the name. An EU-wide approval vote has been pushed from October to at least December.

Why it matters

Musk has called the software central to Tesla's future growth; clearing regulatory objections to the name is a step toward EU-wide approval, which the rebrand alone doesn't guarantee.

The bear case

Changing a label doesn't change the underlying software or resolve the technical sticking points Germany and France are still testing for.

What to watch

The EU-wide vote on the driver-assist software, now expected no earlier than December.

Meta blocks TikTok parent ByteDance's ads in seven countries

The move

Meta banned advertising from ByteDance, TikTok's parent, on Facebook and Instagram, effective Thursday, October 8, in the US, Canada, Egypt, Indonesia, Japan, Thailand and Vietnam. The ban also covers third-party ads directing users to TikTok or other ByteDance properties. A Meta spokesperson said, "We don't have to run ads from a competitor whose goal is to pull people off our apps." The move follows a August settlement in which Meta agreed to daily and nighttime usage limits for minors, after which Meta pressed TikTok to adopt similar limits; TikTok rejected Meta's ads calling for that.

Why it matters

Meta is using ad-platform access, not just price or features, as a competitive weapon against its biggest short-form video rival.

The bear case

The ban could invite antitrust scrutiny over a dominant platform denying a rival access to its ad network, and neither company has disclosed how much ad revenue is actually at stake.

What to watch

Whether Meta extends the ban to more countries or TikTok challenges it.

Berkshire raises Lennar stake past 12% despite short-seller report

The move

Berkshire Hathaway bought about 2.4 million Lennar Class A shares and 15,028 Class B shares, worth roughly $192.6 million, on October 1 and 2, lifting its stake to about 28.43 million Class A and 568,028 Class B shares, per an SEC filing reported October 6. Lennar shares fell sharply after short-seller Hunterbrook published a report on Lennar's dealings with its Millrose Properties spinoff, which it said bought more than 700 Lennar homes for an estimated $200 million-plus, 356 of them in the final week of Lennar's third quarter.

Why it matters

Berkshire kept buying into the stock even as a short-seller report and a weak housing market pressured the shares — a vote of confidence from the company's largest outside shareholder.

The bear case

Berkshire's purchases predate the Hunterbrook report; it's unclear whether Buffett's team would buy at the same pace now, and mortgage rates near 7.3% are still a drag on homebuilders.

What to watch

Lennar's next earnings report and any Berkshire 13F update on whether the buying continued through the Hunterbrook selloff.

Amazon explores $8 billion investor financing for Nvidia chips

The move

Amazon is in talks with outside investors over roughly $8 billion of financing built around its Nvidia chips, the Financial Times reported October 2. Under the plan, the chips would move into a special-purpose vehicle that raises money from investors, with Amazon leasing them back for its data centers. As of the report, no deal had been finalized and Amazon had not commented.

Why it matters

The structure would let Amazon keep using the chips in AWS while shifting some of the capital cost off its own balance sheet, as its 2026 capex is expected to run around $220 billion.

The bear case

Advanced AI chips depreciate fast — Bloomberg has flagged roughly a five-year replacement cycle — so outside investors in the vehicle would bear the risk that newer Nvidia generations erode the hardware's value before the financing is paid off.

What to watch

Whether Amazon and its investors finalize the structure, and whether other hyperscalers adopt similar off-balance-sheet chip financing.
