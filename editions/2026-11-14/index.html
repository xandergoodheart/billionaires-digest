California voters reject billionaire wealth tax

The move

The Associated Press called California's Proposition 40 defeated on Nov. 9, nearly a week after Election Day as mail ballots were counted. The measure would have imposed a one-time 5% tax on the personal assets of residents worth more than $1 billion, with most revenue going to healthcare. A group opposing the measure raised more than $300 million; Google co-founder Sergey Brin gave more than $100 million of that total, the New York Times reported.

Why it matters

It's the clearest sign yet that California billionaires can still out-spend a populist tax measure even in a state that leans heavily Democratic, and it removes, for now, a template other states' wealth-tax backers were watching.

The bear case

The fight isn't fully over: a companion measure to bar future wealth taxes, Proposition 42, was still being counted as a narrower contest, and backers of Prop 40 could try again in a future election.

What to watch

Final certification of both propositions by the California Secretary of State, and whether similar wealth-tax measures appear on other states' 2027 or 2028 ballots.

Berkshire sells ¥182.3 billion in new yen bonds

The move

Berkshire Hathaway priced a five-tranche yen bond offering on Nov. 13 totaling ¥182.3 billion (about $1.2 billion), an SEC filing showed. The notes mature between 2029 and 2056, with coupons from 2.624% to 4.571%, and are rated Aa2/AA. The company will receive about ¥182.0 billion in proceeds; settlement is set for Nov. 19. The filing doesn't state a use of proceeds.

Why it matters

Berkshire has used yen debt for years to help fund its stakes in Japanese trading houses, so a fresh bond sale signals it's still comfortable borrowing cheaply in yen even with its cash pile already near $357 billion.

The bear case

The filing itself doesn't say what the money is for, so any link to new Japanese stock purchases is speculation until Berkshire's next 13F or annual filing shows it.

What to watch

Berkshire's next 13F filing, due by mid-February, for any change in its Japanese trading-house stakes.

Page family trusts file to sell $148 million in Alphabet stock

The move

Three trusts tied to Larry Page — the Lawrence Page Trust, Trust II and Trust III — filed SEC Form 144 notices on Nov. 12 for a combined 507,680 Alphabet shares, split evenly between Class A and Class C stock, worth about $148.3 million at the filings' reference prices. The sales are set under a trading plan the trusts adopted Nov. 30, 2020.

Why it matters

A Form 144 is only a notice of intent to sell under a pre-existing plan, not a confirmed sale, but the combined size shows continued, steady share sales from Page's holding structure even as Alphabet stock has rallied this year.

The bear case

Regular sales under a multi-year 10b5-1-style plan are routine for insiders with large stakes and don't necessarily reflect any view on Alphabet's prospects.

What to watch

Form 4 filings from the same trusts confirming the sales actually went through.

Newsmax swings to profit on a one-time fee

The move

Newsmax reported Nov. 12 that third-quarter revenue rose 52.5% year over year to $69.0 million, which included a one-time $14.0 million service-revenue termination fee; excluding that item, revenue grew 21.6% to $55.0 million. Net income was $15.2 million, versus a $4.1 million loss a year earlier, and adjusted EBITDA was $19.0 million. The company kept its underlying full-year revenue guidance at $212 million to $216 million.

Why it matters

Newsmax, in which Interactive Brokers founder Thomas Peterffy holds roughly a quarter of the shares, needed a cleaner quarter after a post-IPO slide; the swing to profit and maintained guidance give the stock a reason to stabilize.

The bear case

More than a quarter of the quarter's revenue growth came from a one-time fee that won't repeat, and digital revenue actually fell 4.1% even as broadcasting grew.

What to watch

Whether Newsmax's underlying (ex-one-time-item) revenue growth holds up without one-time items in the fourth quarter.

Fast Retailing posts record profit as Uniqlo sales jump

The move

Fast Retailing, the Uniqlo parent chaired by Tadashi Yanai, reported Nov. 10 that revenue for the business year ended in August rose 16.6% to 3.96 trillion yen, with net profit up 25.3% to a record 542.52 billion yen. Yanai said the company's longstanding goal of 10 trillion yen in annual sales is "now within our sights."

Why it matters

The record result shows Uniqlo's international expansion is still outrunning the broader apparel market, at a time several Western retailers have struggled with soft demand.

The bear case

A weak yen flatters yen-denominated results for a company with large overseas sales; the growth rate in constant currency isn't broken out in the release.

What to watch

Fast Retailing's first-quarter results for the new fiscal year, typically reported in early January.

Adelson sells her Mavericks stake to the Dumont family

The move

Patrick and Sivan Dumont bought Miriam Adelson's majority stake in the Dallas Mavericks, giving the Dumonts 69% of the ownership group, ESPN and The Athletic reported; Fox4 and other outlets confirmed the deal this month. Adelson, whose family controls Las Vegas Sands, had held the stake since buying it from Mark Cuban in December 2023; Patrick Dumont, her son-in-law and the Sands' CEO, had served as the team's governor throughout. Cuban keeps his 27% stake and an option to buy up to 20% more from the Dumonts at the original valuation.

Why it matters

It consolidates control of the Mavericks in the hands of the Dumonts, who've run the team day to day since 2023, and ends Adelson's direct ownership stake in an NBA franchise.

The bear case

Neither side has disclosed a price for the shares, so there's no way yet to judge whether Adelson got a good return on her 2023 purchase.

What to watch

Any NBA board of governors approval and whether Cuban exercises his option to buy more of the team.

ByteDance plans a Muse-like AI agent within weeks

The move

ByteDance is preparing to launch an AI video assistant similar to Meta's Muse within weeks, Bloomberg reported Nov. 11, citing people familiar with the plans. At an internal all-hands meeting, co-founder Zhang Yiming told staff ByteDance could compete with leading AI labs "at the cutting edge" but not before 2029 or 2030.

Why it matters

It shows ByteDance racing to match Meta and other rivals in AI video tools even as its own founder sets a long timeline for catching the frontier labs.

The bear case

The report relies on anonymous sources and ByteDance hasn't confirmed a product or date; Bloomberg said the company didn't respond to a request for comment.

What to watch

Whether ByteDance actually ships the assistant in the "coming weeks" window the report described.

Koch's holding company sells more Ibotta stock

The move

KDT Ibotta Holdings, an entity tied to Koch, Inc., sold 275,000 Ibotta shares over Nov. 11-12 for roughly $16.4 million at prices between $58.97 and $60.38, an SEC Form 4 filed Nov. 13 showed. It's the latest in a series of sales from the stake; the same entity sold 200,000 shares over Nov. 9-10 and another 200,000 over Nov. 5-6.

Why it matters

The steady pace of sales — now three filings in two weeks — suggests a systematic wind-down of Koch's position in the cashback-rewards app rather than a one-off trade.

The bear case

Without a disclosed trading plan, it's not possible to say how much of the original stake is left or when the selling might stop.

What to watch

Whether the pace of sales continues at a similar size in the next Form 4 filing.
