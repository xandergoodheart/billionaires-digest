Elon MuskIPO / MarketsAerospaceTSLA · SPCXAI read · ▼ Big bet

Tesla profit falls as Musk weighs SpaceX merger

The move

Tesla's Q3 net income fell 44% to $787 million as revenue stayed flat at $28.05 billion and spending on Optimus and AI rose; free cash flow turned positive at $1.53 billion. A day earlier, SpaceX reported revenue up 161% to $14.6 billion and net income of $4.4 billion, and Musk said on SpaceX's earnings call that both companies have formed special board committees to explore merging them, which would need a majority vote of all Tesla shares.

Why it matters

A combined company could let Musk's businesses share AI, chip and robotics work, and investors have started pricing in that possibility.

The bear case

Musk said the committees haven't agreed on terms and a deal isn't guaranteed; Tesla's own numbers show a core auto business that is shrinking.

What to watch

Tesla must hold its annual meeting by Dec 6, 2026, a possible venue for a shareholder vote on a merger.

Carlos Slim's firm sells $86 million of Talos Energy

The move

Control Empresarial de Capitales, Carlos Slim's holding company, sold about 4.9 million shares of Talos Energy between Oct 15 and Oct 19 for roughly $85.9 million combined, according to SEC filings. The sales cut its stake to 24.5 million shares, or 14.7% of the oil and gas company, from about 29.4 million shares.

Why it matters

Slim's firm has been a major Talos shareholder for years; a steady reduction can signal he is reallocating away from the stock.

The bear case

The filings don't say why the sales happened, and Slim's firm still owns a large, double-digit stake in the company.

What to watch

Future SEC filings from Control Empresarial de Capitales for further changes to its Talos Energy stake.

Mark ZuckerbergOtherAI & techMETAAI read · — Staying put

Meta creates new stock class to keep Zuckerberg in California

The move

Meta's board created a new non-voting Class C stock, paying one Class C share for every Class A or Class B share starting January 2027, according to an SEC filing. Zuckerberg can freely sell 20% of the Class C shares he receives; the rest unlocks gradually over eight years. In exchange, he committed to remain a California resident, or wherever Meta is headquartered, for eight years.

Why it matters

Zuckerberg had told Meta he was weighing a move out of California over its proposed billionaire tax; selling voting shares to cover a tax bill would have cut his control of the company.

The bear case

The arrangement dilutes other shareholders with a new stock class, and the tax it is designed for, Proposition 40, hasn't passed yet.

What to watch

Nov 3, 2026: California voters decide Proposition 40.

Sergey Brin spends over $100 million fighting California tax

The move

Google co-founder Sergey Brin has put more than $100 million into Building a Better California, a group opposing Proposition 40, the state's proposed one-time 5% tax on billionaires' net worth. Voters decide the measure on Nov 3, 2026.

Why it matters

Brin is one of several wealthy Californians bankrolling the opposition; estimates suggest his own tax bill could run into the billions if it passes.

The bear case

Supporters of the measure, including several Nobel-winning economists, say it would raise billions for the state and argue opponents are overstating the risk of billionaires leaving.

What to watch

Nov 3, 2026: California voters decide Proposition 40.

Ambani's Jio Platforms IPO fully subscribed within two hours

The move

Jio Platforms, Reliance Industries' telecom unit, drew enough bids to cover its entire $3.2 billion IPO within two hours of opening on Oct 21, according to BSE exchange data. The offer covers 197.5 million shares priced between 1,065 and 1,119 rupees each; Jio is expected to list on Oct 28.

Larry EllisonIPO / MarketsAI & techORCLAI read · ▼ Debt worries

Oracle's AI debt faces a stress test as bonds wobble

The move

Oracle's credit default swaps, a gauge of how risky bond investors see the company, have swung sharply over the past week. The company's S&P credit rating sits at BBB-, the lowest investment-grade level, after a July downgrade, and Oracle has issued $118 billion in long-term debt to help fund its AI data center buildout, including a large commitment tied to OpenAI.

Why it matters

Investors are worried Oracle is borrowing heavily to fund AI data centers just as a competitor, CoreWeave, showed signs of debt stress this month.

The bear case

Oracle says it remains focused on disciplined capital allocation and preserving its investment-grade rating, and some analysts say they aren't concerned about its debt levels.

What to watch

Whether S&P or other raters cut Oracle's rating further toward junk.

Jeff BezosInvestmentAerospacePrivateAI read · ▲ Scaling up

Blue Origin plans new Florida launch pads for New Glenn

The move

Blue Origin announced plans on Oct 16 for two more launch pads at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, on top of two already under construction, aiming to support multiple New Glenn flights a week by the end of the decade. The company is also building a $330 million upper-stage factory at Rocket Park, targeted for mid-2027, and adding two more recovery ships.

Why it matters

Blue Origin is racing to scale up launches after a May explosion damaged one of its pads, and wants more capacity before New Glenn returns to flight.

The bear case

Neither new pad is built yet, the company is approved for only 12 launches a year while it has requested 50, and a federal environmental review is still underway.

What to watch

Blue Origin says its New Glenn rocket is expected to return to flight in December 2026.

Jensen HuangOtherAI & techNVDAAI read · ▼ Funding gap

Jensen Huang says AI boom's bottleneck is now capital

The move

Speaking to CNBC at GTC Berlin on Oct 22, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said demand from 'neocloud' data-center operators is 'off the charts' but that funding, not customers, is now the constraint. He said Nvidia wants to establish its compute as a 'financeable asset' that lenders can treat as collateral, building on a $500 billion AI financing plan Nvidia announced in August with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR. Nvidia shares fell as much as 2% in premarket trading that day, and neocloud peers Nebius, IREN and Applied Digital all fell further.

Why it matters

If Huang is right that financing, not demand, is the bottleneck, it suggests the AI buildout could keep going as long as lenders keep extending credit.

The bear case

Bond investors have already pushed up borrowing costs for AI-heavy companies like CoreWeave and Oracle, and two banks have issued negative notes on CoreWeave this week.

What to watch

Whether more lenders agree to treat AI compute as loan collateral.
