Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns effective immediately

The move

Nvidia's board said Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and a board member on Nov. 12, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress agreed to serve as interim CEO while keeping her finance role, and Harvey Jones was named the company's first-ever board chair. The announcement gave no reason for the departure. The board said it is searching for a permanent CEO and will consider internal and external candidates.

Why it matters

Huang built Nvidia from a graphics-chip maker into the world's most valuable company and the main supplier to the AI boom; his sudden exit leaves the industry's biggest chipmaker without a clear successor.

The bear case

Bulls note Kress and the existing leadership team can keep operations steady, and that Nvidia's order backlog doesn't depend on one executive.

What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17, its first since Huang's resignation.

Musk, Tobias Lütke back a push for Huang's return

The move

A 'Stand with Jensen' petition asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang had drawn more than 170,000 signatures as of Nov. 15, with Elon Musk and Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke among the public signers. The Financial Times reported Huang has discussed a possible return with close confidants; Nvidia says it hasn't formally engaged any CEO candidates and is still picking a search firm. The FT separately reported the board has approached Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas as a possible permanent successor.

Why it matters

Backers argue Nvidia's AI business is tied closely to Huang's own relationships with chipmakers and cloud customers, making a clean leadership swap harder than usual.

The bear case

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for 'a dramatic shakeup' and an outside hire, and analyst Dan Ives said he sees 'no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO.'

What to watch

Nvidia's board investigation into Huang's conduct is still open, with no timeline given for naming a permanent CEO.

AMD, Broadcom tumble as chip rotation follows Huang's exit

The move

AMD shares fell 8.75% and Broadcom fell 4.76% on Nov. 13 as investors rotated out of chipmakers following Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation; Nvidia itself fell about 2% the same day. Broadcom had gained 24% and AMD about 30% over the prior week, per CNBC.

Why it matters

The sudden leadership vacuum atop Nvidia added fresh uncertainty to the broader AI trade, and some investors shifted money into other chip names rather than exiting the sector outright.

The bear case

Some of the move likely reflects profit-taking after a strong week rather than any change at AMD or Broadcom themselves.

What to watch

Broadcom reports fiscal fourth-quarter results on Dec. 9, 2026.

SoftBank posts $7B loss as Arm slides, US AI bet backfires

The move

SoftBank Group reported a net loss of about 1.1 trillion yen (roughly $7 billion) for its fiscal second quarter (July-September 2026), versus a 2.5 trillion yen profit a year earlier that was driven by gains on its OpenAI stake. The loss included a $3.7 billion hit from advance payments for US data-center power equipment, including a Pike County, Ohio project, after plans changed; SoftBank says it is entitled to rebates of up to $2.1 billion on future purchases. Financing costs more than doubled to 462 billion yen, and SoftBank's margin loan against its Arm stake grew by $5 billion to about $25 billion as Arm shares fell.

Why it matters

SoftBank's results are closely tied to Arm, which it controls, and to its bets on US AI infrastructure; both moved against it this quarter.

The bear case

SoftBank raised a record $56.5 billion in capital during the same quarter and booked a $1.3 billion gain on its ByteDance stake, and much of the loss is non-cash mark-to-market moves rather than operating losses.

What to watch

SoftBank's CFO said an announcement on OpenAI's next funding round, reported to value it at $1.25 trillion, is highly likely this quarter.

Newsmax posts record revenue, swings to a profit

The move

Newsmax reported third-quarter revenue of $69.0 million, up 52.5% from $45.3 million a year earlier, including a one-time $14.0 million contract-termination fee; excluding that item, revenue rose 21.6% to $55.0 million. Net income was $15.2 million versus a $4.1 million net loss a year ago, and adjusted EBITDA was $19.0 million versus a $0.9 million loss. The network said it reached 22.5 million total viewers in the quarter and ranked fourth among cable news channels. Newsmax maintained its full-year underlying revenue guidance of $212 million to $216 million.

Why it matters

The swing to profitability comes as Newsmax, in which Thomas Peterffy is an investor, keeps building cable-news audience share.

The bear case

Stripping out the one-time termination fee, underlying adjusted EBITDA was just $5.0 million, a thinner margin than the headline numbers suggest.

What to watch

Newsmax's full-year 2026 guidance, including the one-time item, is $226 million to $230 million in revenue.

Arista co-founder sells $43M more stock under trading plan

The move

Arista Networks co-founder Andreas von Bechtolsheim sold 260,000 shares on Nov. 5 for about $43.0 million, at prices between $158.35 and $171.55, in 14 lots under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan he entered on Feb. 20, 2026; the Form 4 was accepted Nov. 9. That brings his total since May 22 to 14 sales, 3,784,740 shares and about $668.2 million.

Why it matters

The sale continues a selling program set up months in advance, not a reaction to any single event at Arista.

The bear case

Because the plan was scheduled in advance, the timing says little about Bechtolsheim's current view of the stock, and Arista shares have recovered some of their early-November losses since.

Congress presses Huang for closed-door espionage briefing

The move

Republican chairs of three House committees asked Huang for a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18, covering Nvidia's defenses against foreign espionage and his contact with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive the letter describes as a Chinese intelligence asset not related to him; Huang had until Nov. 11 to confirm or propose another date. Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation on Nov. 8, led by independent directors and outside counsel, saying it has 'no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.' In a memo to staff, Huang wrote, 'I was unfaithful to my wife.'

Why it matters

Lawmakers from both parties say they need to determine 'the extent to which U.S. national security has been placed at risk' given Nvidia's central role in the AI chip supply chain.

The bear case

Nvidia's board says ordinary business information shared with regional cloud customers does not include sensitive trade secrets or anything relevant to national security.

What to watch

The requested closed-door briefing was set for Nov. 18, one day after Nvidia's earnings report.
