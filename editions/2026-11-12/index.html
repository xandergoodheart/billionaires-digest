Nvidia board investigates Huang over spying claims

The move

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Nov. 8 after CEO Jensen Huang told directors he had a personal relationship with a businesswoman tied to Nvidia customer Megaspeed, whom U.S. intelligence has said was gathering information for Beijing. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and a company spokesperson said Huang remains committed to continuing as CEO without interruption. The stock fell 4.3% on Nov. 9 and 5.5% on Nov. 10, erasing almost $200 billion in value over the two sessions.

Why it matters

A CEO's personal conduct becoming a national-security question is rare, and the stock reaction shows investors are treating it as a real governance risk, not noise.

The bear case

The board says no secrets were compromised and Huang isn't going anywhere, so the selloff could prove overdone once the investigation wraps up.

What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter earnings on Nov. 17; the board's investigation, run with outside counsel, has no stated deadline.

Warren BuffettInvestmentFinanceBRK.BAI read · ▲ Buffett's team keeps buying back, not spending the pile

Berkshire's profit drops as buybacks speed up

The move

Berkshire Hathaway reported Q3 operating earnings of $12.7 billion, down 5.8% from a year earlier, and net earnings of $22.1 billion, down 28%, mainly on smaller investment gains. The company bought back $9.6 billion of its own stock during the quarter. BNSF railroad earnings rose 19% to $1.73 billion, while insurance underwriting fell 43% to $1.35 billion. Berkshire's cash and short-term investments stood near $352 billion to $377 billion, depending on how Treasury bills bought in September are counted.

Why it matters

Leaning harder on buybacks than acquisitions is a sign the company still isn't finding deals big enough to justify its cash pile.

The bear case

A cash pile that size sitting mostly in short-term bills is also a bet that stocks are too expensive right now, which could look wrong if the market keeps climbing.

What to watch

Berkshire's next 10-Q and any update on how the cash pile gets deployed heading into 2027.

Masayoshi SonInvestmentAI & techSFTBYAI read · ▼ The AI infrastructure bet gets expensive

SoftBank posts rare loss on AI data center bet

The move

SoftBank Group reported a net loss of about 1.12 trillion yen ($7.1 billion) for the July-September quarter, compared with a 2.5 trillion yen profit a year earlier. The loss included a $3.7 billion writedown on advance payments for power equipment for U.S. AI data center projects after plans changed, plus financing costs that more than doubled to 462 billion yen. Shares fell as much as 10.5% in Tokyo trading after the report.

Why it matters

This is SoftBank's first loss in nearly two years, and it shows the real cost of Son's AI infrastructure bets when a project doesn't go as planned.

The bear case

One project writedown isn't the same as the broader AI bet turning bad, and SoftBank has absorbed bigger swings before.

What to watch

Whether SoftBank discloses further writedowns on its other U.S. data center commitments next quarter.

Broadcom joins Anthropic's $26 billion private placement

The move

Broadcom shares rose 3.9% on Nov. 10 after an amended Anthropic SEC filing named it as a buyer of roughly $1.0 billion of non-voting Class N shares in Anthropic's $26 billion private placement tied to its IPO. Other named buyers include Google, Nvidia, AMD, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC.

Why it matters

Buying into the company it already supplies chips to ties Broadcom's fortunes even more tightly to Anthropic's success.

The bear case

A $1 billion stake is small next to Broadcom's other Anthropic commitments, including a chip-financing facility worth tens of billions, so this looks more symbolic than strategic.

What to watch

Anthropic's IPO pricing and whether the other named buyers' stakes are confirmed in the final prospectus.

Tesla launches Robotaxi service in San Antonio

The move

Tesla began offering Robotaxi rides in San Antonio starting at 5 a.m. local time on Nov. 9, its fifth Texas city, joining a service that also covers Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston and limited areas of Miami and Orlando. Tesla has not disclosed the San Antonio service area's size, hours of operation, or whether human safety monitors are aboard. Waymo already runs a robotaxi service in San Antonio, which it restarted in October after a weather-related pause.

Why it matters

Adding cities quickly, even with few disclosed details, is Tesla's way of showing investors that unsupervised driving is scaling.

The bear case

Waymo already operates in all the same Texas cities Tesla just entered, so Tesla is matching a rival's footprint there, not leading it.

What to watch

Whether Tesla discloses San Antonio fleet size and safety-monitor status, and its next city announcement.

Sundar PichaiProduct launchAI & techGOOGLAI read · — A fourth TPU launch in one year

Google unveils two new TPU chips for AI

The move

Google introduced two new eighth-generation TPU chips on Nov. 9: the TPU 8i Max, which pairs two TPU 8i chips for double the compute, memory and bandwidth of a single TPU 8i, and the TPU 8tx, built with AMD for confidential computing during AI inference. Google said this is the first year it has introduced four TPU systems. The company did not disclose pricing or a customer availability date.

Why it matters

Four TPU launches in one year shows Google pushing hard to give AI customers an alternative to Nvidia's chips.

The bear case

Nvidia's CUDA software remains the industry standard, and Google itself still runs plenty of Nvidia GPUs alongside its own chips, so this is additive, not a replacement.

What to watch

When Google sets pricing and availability for the new chips, and how much TPU revenue it reports this quarter.

Stephen SchwarzmanDivestitureFinanceCRBGAI read · ▼ Trimming a legacy insurance stake

Blackstone-linked holder sells $110 million of Corebridge

The move

Corebridge Financial agreed on Nov. 6 to repurchase about $110 million of its own stock directly from Argon Holdco LLC, a holding company whose sole member is a Blackstone affiliate tied to Stephen Schwarzman. The deal was priced at $34.09 a share, the stock's Nov. 6 closing price, and was set to close Nov. 10. A related Form 4 shows Argon Holdco still held about 44.2 million Corebridge shares after the sale.

Why it matters

Selling shares directly back to the company, rather than on the open market, let Blackstone trim its stake without pressuring the stock price.

The bear case

Blackstone still holds tens of millions of Corebridge shares, so this sale is a trim, not an exit.

What to watch

Whether Blackstone's Argon Holdco strikes further repurchase agreements with Corebridge as it continues reducing its stake.

Larry EllisonIPO / MarketsMediaPSKYAI read · ▼ Debt overhang clouds a decent quarter

Paramount Skydance profit misses despite revenue beat

The move

Paramount Skydance reported Q3 2026 revenue of $7 billion, up 5%, but profit of $73 million, or 6 cents a share, well below the 20 cents analysts expected. The company, now carrying its Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, has nearly $80 billion in net debt and targets a 3x leverage ratio by 2029 without raising new capital or selling assets. Shares fell more than 2% in after-hours trading on Nov. 9 and were down 7.6% by the close on Nov. 10.

Why it matters

A revenue beat getting overshadowed by an earnings miss and a near-$80 billion debt load shows investors focused on how the Warner Bros. Discovery deal gets paid for, not just grown into.

The bear case

Streaming and studio profits both grew double digits this quarter, which is the part of the business the debt is meant to fund.

What to watch

The company's first combined quarterly report with Warner Bros. Discovery, expected in early February 2027.

Bechtolsheim sells another $44 million of Arista stock

The move

Arista Networks chairman Andreas von Bechtolsheim sold 260,000 shares on Nov. 6 for about $43.9 million, per a Form 4 filed Nov. 10. It follows a similar sale of 260,000 shares on Nov. 5 for about $43.0 million, filed Nov. 9. Together the two filings cover 520,000 shares sold for roughly $87 million.

Why it matters

Back-to-back sales of this size are usually arranged well in advance, but the total still shows an insider taking a large sum off the table.

The bear case

Founders and executives sell under pre-arranged trading plans routinely for diversification, and it isn't necessarily a signal about the company's prospects.

What to watch

Whether Bechtolsheim's selling pace continues in Arista's next round of Form 4 filings.
