Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia's CEO

The move

Nvidia's board said Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and a director effective immediately on Nov. 12. The board named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO; she keeps her finance role while the company searches for a permanent successor. The board also named Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys CEO, as Nvidia's first-ever board chair.

Why it matters

Huang built Nvidia into the center of the AI boom over more than three decades; losing him without warning raises succession and strategy questions heading into earnings and a fight in Washington over China chip exports.

The bear case

Nvidia says Kress and the leadership team have the board's full confidence and that operations and product plans continue without interruption; the stock has stayed near record levels this year.

What to watch

Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17 at 1:20 p.m. PT, its first earnings call since Huang's exit.

Congress presses Huang to testify on China ties

The move

House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast and other House Republicans asked Jensen Huang to brief Congress behind closed doors over his relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Nvidia partner Megaspeed whom U.S. authorities suspect of spying for China. Huang is expected to testify before Thanksgiving, Nov. 26; Nvidia was in talks this week with congressional staff over a date and location.

Why it matters

The inquiry adds a national-security dimension to Huang's sudden departure and could shape upcoming restrictions on advanced chip exports to China.

The bear case

Neither Megaspeed nor Alice Huang has been charged with a crime, and China has denied the spying allegation; Nvidia says it has followed export-control rules.

What to watch

A scheduled date and location for Huang's testimony, expected before Nov. 26.

AMD slides as chip-stock rally reverses

The move

AMD shares fell sharply on Nov. 13 in a broad chip-sector reversal that followed Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia's CEO two days earlier. Our pricing data shows AMD closed down 8.8% that day.

Why it matters

AMD had rallied as investors rotated out of Nvidia after Huang's exit; the reversal shows how closely chipmakers' stocks are now tied to Nvidia-specific headlines rather than their own results.

The bear case

AMD's own guidance is unchanged: Su has told investors she expects 2027 revenue growth above 80%, with data-center AI revenue more than doubling.

What to watch

AMD's next quarterly update for whether its MI400/MI450 chip ramp stays on track.

Dell falls on report of AI server smuggling probe

The move

Dell shares fell about 7.6% in afternoon trading on Nov. 13, to roughly $343, after a report that federal investigators are examining AI server manufacturers over alleged smuggling of chips, a story that also weighed on Super Micro Computer and Nvidia that day.

Why it matters

Dell depends heavily on AI server sales to data centers, so scrutiny of the AI hardware supply chain hits the stock hard on its own, beyond the broader chip selloff.

The bear case

No charges have been reported against Dell itself, and the company has guided to about $19 billion of AI server revenue this quarter.

What to watch

The move

Arm Holdings shares fell on Nov. 13 amid the broader chip-sector pullback and reports that Nvidia's board had approached Arm CEO Rene Haas about replacing Jensen Huang. SoftBank, led by Masayoshi Son, controls Arm. Our pricing data shows Arm closed down 4.6% that day.

Larry Page trusts file to sell $148M of Alphabet

The move

Three trusts tied to Larry Page filed Form 144 notices after the close on Nov. 12 to sell 253,840 Class A and 253,840 Class C Alphabet shares, worth about $148.3 million combined, through J.P. Morgan Securities. The filings cite a trading plan adopted Nov. 30, 2020.

Why it matters

The sale is part of a long-running, pre-arranged plan rather than a new decision, but it is one of the larger Page family filings this year.

The bear case

A Form 144 is only a notice of intent to sell, not a confirmed trade; the actual amount could differ once Form 4s are filed.

What to watch

Form 4 filings confirming the completed trade, expected around Nov. 16.

Warren presses Barra over GM's $20B buyback

The move

Sen. Elizabeth Warren sent GM chair and CEO Mary Barra a letter dated Nov. 12 criticizing GM's $20 billion stock buyback plan, arguing it enriches executives and shareholders after GM raised prices and laid off or furloughed thousands of workers. Warren urged GM to use an expected roughly $500 million tariff refund for workers and customers instead.

Why it matters

The letter adds political pressure to GM's capital-return plans just as the company leans on buybacks to support its share price heading into what it has called a "bumpy 2027."

The bear case

GM has framed the buyback as a sign of confidence in its business, and the letter carries no legal force on its own.

What to watch

Whether GM issues a public response to Warren's letter.

Amancio Ortega collects record Inditex dividend

The move

Amancio Ortega, who owns 59.29% of Inditex through his holding companies, received the second of two equal installments of Inditex's 2025 dividend this month. Inditex paid a total of €3.234 billion to shareholders for the year, split between May and November 2026.

Why it matters

The payout lands as Inditex shares have traded above €60 for the first time, pushing the value of Ortega's stake further into record territory.

The bear case

Most of the payout simply reflects Ortega's existing 59% stake rather than new wealth creation, and the share price could still give back its 2026 gains.

What to watch

Inditex's next trading update for whether the stock holds above €60.
