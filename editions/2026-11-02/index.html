Brin and allies pour $102 million into beating a wealth tax

The move

Sergey Brin has put $102 million into Building a Better California, a campaign group he founded in January, to help defeat Proposition 40, a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of Californians worth more than $1 billion who lived in the state on Jan. 1, 2026. The measure, sponsored by the SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West and backed by Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Ro Khanna, goes to voters on Nov. 3. Through Oct. 20, opponents had spent more than $304 million fighting Prop 40 and backing two counter-measures, Props 41 and 42, which could block the tax even if it passes. Other named contributors include Eric Schmidt, John Doerr, Patrick Collison and Michael Moritz. A Politico analysis found more than 70 current and former California billionaires have directed a record $612 million into state races this cycle.

Why it matters

A yes vote could cost Brin and other ultra-wealthy Californians a one-time payment worth billions combined, and a win for the opposition could become a template for beating similar wealth-tax pushes in other states.

The bear case

Backers say the tax hits only a few thousand people once and could raise tens of billions of dollars for healthcare, and some polling has shown public support for taxing billionaires.

What to watch

The Nov. 3 vote on Propositions 40, 41 and 42.

Jio Platforms lists up 21% in India's biggest-ever IPO

The move

Jio Platforms, the Reliance Industries telecom and digital unit run by Mukesh Ambani, began trading on India's NSE and BSE on Oct. 28 after raising about $3.1 billion in India's largest-ever IPO. Shares listed at 1,358 rupees, a premium of more than 21% to the 1,119-rupee issue price, before rising further to list up as much as 25% on the day, giving the company a market value of about 13.05 trillion rupees ($137.1 billion) -- comparable to AT&T's and Verizon's, per LSEG data. Reliance Industries still owns more than 64% of Jio Platforms after the offering.

Why it matters

The listing cements Jio as one of India's most valuable companies and gives Ambani's conglomerate a newly tradable stock to use for future deals and fundraising.

The bear case

Analysts quoted by CNBC said much of the good news is already priced in after the listing-day jump, with the stock trading at roughly 34 times earnings.

What to watch

Jio Platforms' first quarterly results as a public company.

Larry PageIPO / MarketsAI & techGOOGLAI read · ▼ Earnings miss

Alphabet shares drop 8% despite record revenue

The move

Alphabet, where co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin remain large shareholders, reported Q3 revenue of $127.6 billion on Oct. 28, up 25% from a year earlier and just short of the roughly $128 billion analysts expected. Google Cloud revenue roughly doubled to $30.7 billion, up 103%, with its operating margin rising to 36.2% from 23.7%. Net income fell 25% to $26.2 billion, as an $8.7 billion loss on equity investments cut diluted earnings per share to $2.12 from $2.87, well below the nearly $3.00 Wall Street expected. Capital spending rose 114% to $51.4 billion for the quarter, pushing free cash flow to negative $9.1 billion. Shares fell 8.25% in after-hours trading, testing the $300 level.

Why it matters

It's a reminder that heavy AI spending and volatile investment gains can swing Alphabet's bottom line even when its core advertising and cloud businesses are growing fast.

The bear case

Revenue and cloud growth both topped last year's pace, and the earnings miss was driven mainly by a one-time investment loss rather than a change in the underlying business.

What to watch

Whether Alphabet's capex keeps climbing when it reports Q4 results.

Steve BallmerIPO / MarketsAI & techMSFTAI read · ▲ Cloud strength

Microsoft tops estimates as Azure growth hits 48%

The move

Microsoft, where Steve Ballmer and Bill Gates remain major shareholders, reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $92.3 billion on Oct. 28, up 19% from a year earlier and above the roughly $90.7 billion analysts expected. Azure revenue grew 48% to about $33.2 billion, an acceleration from 43% growth the prior quarter, and Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 30% to $63.9 billion. Net income rose 32% to $36.6 billion, or $4.92 a share; excluding the effect of its OpenAI investment, non-GAAP net income rose 18% to $36.4 billion. Capital spending on property and equipment nearly doubled to $39.4 billion, and commercial remaining performance obligation -- contracted future revenue -- rose 73% to $677 billion.

Why it matters

Azure's acceleration shows Microsoft's cloud business is still gaining speed even as the company ramps up AI-related spending.

The bear case

Capex nearly doubling from a year ago means Microsoft needs that growth to keep paying off, and the company has guided to capex topping $50 billion again next quarter.

What to watch

Whether Azure growth reaches the 49% to 50% range Microsoft has guided to for next quarter.

Amazon shares fall over 6% despite record margin

The move

Amazon, where Jeff Bezos remains the largest individual shareholder, reported Q3 net sales of $201.9 billion on Oct. 29, up 12% from a year earlier but just under the $202.2 billion analysts expected, with earnings of $1.89 a share versus the $1.97 forecast, per FactSet. AWS sales rose 39% to $46.0 billion, and operating income reached $28.1 billion, a record 13.9% operating margin companywide. Net income fell to $20.7 billion from $21.2 billion, largely because the year-earlier quarter included a $9.5 billion gain on Amazon's Anthropic investment. Shares fell more than 6% in after-hours trading, and Amazon's fourth-quarter sales guidance of $240 billion to $247 billion also came in below the roughly $244.2 billion analysts expected.

Why it matters

AWS posting its fastest growth in years shows cloud demand for AI is still strong, but the stock drop shows investors are getting pickier about even a small miss.

The bear case

The miss was by cents on earnings per share and a fraction of a percent on sales out of $202 billion -- a rounding error next to AWS's acceleration.

What to watch

Whether AWS growth holds above 39% as new data centers come online.

Arista falls 8.5% ahead of Nov. 3 earnings

The move

Arista Networks, co-founded by Andreas Bechtolsheim, fell 8.5% on Oct. 30 amid a broader technology-sector selloff, according to TradingKey. The drop shows how sensitive highly-valued AI-infrastructure stocks have become to broad market caution, even without company-specific bad news.

The bear case

The move came with no new information about Arista's own business, and investors will get a clearer read when the company reports earnings on Nov. 3.

What to watch

Samsung posts record profit on AI memory demand

The move

Samsung Electronics, led by Executive Chairman Jay Y. Lee, reported record results on Oct. 29: third-quarter consolidated revenue of 195.3 trillion won and operating profit of 107.4 trillion won, its highest-ever quarterly operating profit and the first time a Korean company has topped 100 trillion won in operating profit in a single quarter, per the company's own announcement. Samsung said its Memory Business, part of the Device Solutions division, set an all-time high for quarterly revenue and profit, which analysts tied to strong demand for AI memory chips.

Why it matters

It shows how much of the current AI boom's profits are flowing to the handful of companies, like Samsung, that make the memory chips AI data centers need.

The bear case

Samsung's smartphone division was expected to post a second straight quarterly operating loss, a reminder that the AI chip boom hasn't lifted every part of the company.

What to watch

Samsung's Q4 guidance and whether AI memory-chip demand holds into 2027.

Warren BuffettInsider tradeFinanceDVAAI read · ▼ Routine trim

Berkshire trims DaVita stake under buyback-cap deal

The move

A Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary and two of its pension plans sold 382,563 DaVita shares on Oct. 27 at $180.4305 each, worth about $69 million, according to a Form 4 signed by Warren Buffett and filed with the SEC on Oct. 29. The sale left Berkshire's reported indirect stake at 28,314,666 DaVita shares. Buffett, as Berkshire's controlling shareholder, disclaims beneficial ownership of the shares except to the extent of his pecuniary interest in them. Berkshire has sold DaVita shares in similar increments through the year under a long-standing agreement that caps its ownership stake as DaVita buys back its own stock.

Why it matters

It's a reminder that even a stake tied to Buffett can shrink automatically through a contractual mechanism rather than a change of view on the stock.

The bear case

Berkshire's DaVita stake remains large, and these sales are mechanical, required by the buyback-cap agreement rather than a signal Berkshire wants out.

What to watch

DaVita's next buyback disclosure and the resulting size of Berkshire's stake.
