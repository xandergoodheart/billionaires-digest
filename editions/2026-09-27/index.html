Ellison pledges $9.2B more Oracle stock for Warner Bros. bid

The move

An Oracle filing revealed Friday that Larry Ellison pledged 67 million more Oracle shares as collateral for personal loans, a 19% increase from a year earlier. His total pledge is now about 413 million shares, 36% of his 1.16 billion Oracle shares, worth roughly $9.2 billion more than last year at Oracle's Sept. 25 close of $137.10. Oracle bars other officers and directors from pledging shares this way but carves out an exception for Ellison. The borrowed money helps fund his family's commitment to son David Ellison's $111 billion bid for Warner Bros. Discovery through Paramount Skydance, which recently cleared lawsuits from state attorneys general and the Writers Guild of America.

Why it matters

The bigger the pledge, the bigger the risk if Oracle's stock drops sharply: lenders could force a sale of shares to cover the loans, adding selling pressure at the worst time.

The bear case

Ellison's pledged stake is still well under half his Oracle holdings, and the collateral pool is a small slice of his roughly $184 billion net worth, so a forced sale stays unlikely unless Oracle shares fall hard.

What to watch

Whether Paramount Skydance's $111 billion Warner Bros. Discovery bid closes without further legal challenges.

Meta stock jumps 25% since Zuckerberg unveiled Muse AI

The move

At Meta Connect on Sept. 24, Mark Zuckerberg unveiled the Muse Charm, a keychain-sized pendant with a fingerprint sensor that lets people talk to Meta's AI agent without opening a phone app, due to ship in December with no price given yet. He also said Meta will offer more than 100 styles of AI glasses by the end of 2026, including a $1,299 pair launching next spring and $349 camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses. Meta shares rose 4.5% that Thursday and are up more than 25% since Meta's Muse AI agent launched earlier in September.

Why it matters

Zuckerberg is racing to put an AI assistant into a physical device before OpenAI does, betting that hardware, not just apps, will decide who wins the AI-assistant race.

The bear case

Meta gave no price or firm ship date for the Muse Charm beyond "December," and its smart glasses line has already drawn privacy criticism over hidden-camera recording.

What to watch

The Muse Charm's December launch, its price, and whether privacy concerns slow smart-glasses sales.

Berkshire nearly doubles its stake in homebuilder Lennar

The move

Berkshire Hathaway disclosed Friday that it now owns about 25.9 million Lennar shares, worth roughly $2.1 billion and equal to 10.9% of the homebuilder, after buying roughly $349 million more stock between Sept. 17 and Sept. 26. That's nearly double the 13.4 million shares Berkshire held as of June 30, and the purchase pushed Berkshire's stake past the 10% threshold that triggers mandatory SEC disclosure. Lennar shares were down 20.1% for the year and had fallen 9.2% for the week when Berkshire kept buying.

Why it matters

Berkshire is betting on a longer-term recovery in the struggling U.S. housing market, buying a homebuilder while its stock is beaten down rather than waiting for a turnaround to become obvious.

The bear case

Even after nearly doubling, the $2.1 billion Lennar position is still a small stake for a company Berkshire's size, and homebuilder stocks could stay weak if mortgage rates don't come down.

What to watch

Whether Berkshire keeps adding to the Lennar stake, and any change in its size in Berkshire's next quarterly 13F filing.

Arnault family plans to fold LVMH holding companies into one

The move

LVMH's parent Christian Dior said Sept. 23 that the Arnault family plans to merge Financière Agache into Agache, then merge Agache into Christian Dior, and convert Christian Dior into a partnership limited by shares renamed Agache SCA. The resulting single entity would hold 49.76% of LVMH's share capital and 65.55% of its voting rights. Shareholder votes are set for December 2026, with the mergers completing by year-end and a mandatory cash tender offer for minority shareholders, at 95% of Christian Dior's net asset value, running in the first quarter of 2027.

Why it matters

Bernard Arnault, 77, is simplifying the family's multi-layered holding structure into one listed vehicle to keep control of LVMH locked in across generations as succession questions grow louder.

The bear case

The restructuring changes who holds the controlling stake, not LVMH's underlying business, which is still working through a nearly 40% share-price slump this year and weak demand for Louis Vuitton in China.

What to watch

The December 2026 shareholder votes and the tender offer expected in the first quarter of 2027.

Gautam AdaniInvestmentIndustrialsPrivateAI read · ▲ Long-term bet

Adani pledges ₹1 lakh crore for West Bengal over a decade

The move

During a visit to Kolkata on Sept. 25, Gautam Adani said Adani Group will invest ₹1 lakh crore (about $11.3 billion) in West Bengal over the next decade, covering ports, logistics, power generation and distribution, roads, ropeways, green cement and hyper-scale data centers. He also laid the foundation for a ₹4,000-crore, 2,000-bed hospital, Adani Arogya Mandir, in New Town, with 1,000 beds set aside for affordable treatment, plus funding to restore Kolkata's historic Writers' Building and the Kumartuli potters' ghats.

Why it matters

It's one of Adani's largest single-state commitments and signals a push into a state his group has historically had less presence in, spanning both infrastructure and social investment like healthcare.

The bear case

A decade-long pledge across many sectors is easy to announce and hard to hold to; the real test is how much of the ₹1 lakh crore actually gets deployed on the promised timeline.

What to watch

Progress on the West Bengal projects, including the hospital and data-center buildout, through 2035.

Dangote's refinery lifts Nigeria's petrol exports sixfold

The move

Nigeria's National Bureau of Statistics reported that petrol exports hit $750 million in the first half of 2026, a sixfold jump from $64 million in the same quarter last year, driven mainly by Aliko Dangote's refinery moving past earlier outages and African buyers turning to Lagos after regional supply disruptions. A year earlier, Nigeria was still a net petrol importer, buying $1.32 billion of fuel abroad in the first quarter of 2025.

Why it matters

It marks the clearest sign yet that Dangote's $20 billion mega-refinery has moved from a shaky startup into a facility reliable enough to supply other countries, not just Nigeria.

The bear case

The export surge is partly due to temporary regional supply disruptions rather than Dangote's refinery alone, so the sixfold jump may not hold once other regional refiners recover.

What to watch

The Sept. 30 groundbreaking on Dangote's roughly $20 billion Kenya refinery project in Lamu.

Mukesh AmbaniIPO / MarketsOtherRELIANCE.NSAI read · — Rate timing

Reliance lines up second $1 billion rupee bond sale in weeks

The move

Reliance Industries is planning to raise 100 billion rupees (about $1.04 billion) through 10-year rupee bonds at a 7.90% coupon, with bids expected within one to two weeks, Bloomberg reported Sept. 24. It follows an earlier sale of 120 billion rupees (about $1.25 billion) of 5-year notes at 7.47% roughly two weeks earlier. Together the two sales add up to about $2.3 billion in new rupee debt in one month, taking Reliance's outstanding bonds to 540 billion rupees.

Why it matters

Reliance is racing to lock in local borrowing costs before the Reserve Bank of India's Oct. 7 policy decision, at a time when rupee funding has gotten cheaper than dollar debt as U.S. Treasury yields rise.

The bear case

Taking on $2.3 billion in new debt in a single month adds to Reliance's liabilities regardless of how favorable today's rates look, and the bet only pays off if the RBI doesn't cut rates further than expected.

What to watch

Whether Reliance's bond sale prices before the Reserve Bank of India's Oct. 7 policy decision.

Elon MuskProduct launchAerospaceSPCXAI read · — High-stakes test

SpaceX stacks Starship for its first attempt to reach orbit

The move

SpaceX stacked its Ship 41 upper stage atop Super Heavy booster B21 at Starbase, Texas on Sept. 23, forming a 124-meter rocket for Starship's Flight 14, the vehicle's first attempt to reach orbit. The mission would deploy 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites over about a 10-hour flight, far longer than the roughly one-hour suborbital hops of earlier Starship flights. SpaceX is targeting a launch window opening as early as 7:15 a.m. CDT on Sept. 28, pending FAA approval; a temporary flight restriction is in place through Oct. 7.

Why it matters

An actual orbital attempt, rather than another suborbital hop, is the milestone SpaceX needs to start using Starship for real missions like Starlink deployment and, eventually, crewed flights.

The bear case

Earlier Starship test flights have ended in explosions or lost vehicles, and the FAA has not yet granted final approval for a vehicle this large to fly an orbital profile.

What to watch

The Flight 14 launch window opening Sept. 28, and whether the FAA grants approval in time.
