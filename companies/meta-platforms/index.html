Recent moves

Sep 29, 2026 edition
Mark ZuckerbergProduct launchAI & techMETAAI read · ▲ Big swing

Meta launches a new AI platform for businesses

The move

Meta announced Meta Enterprise Platform on Sept. 28, a new division offering companies its AI models, the Muse agent, Meta Business Agent and related tools. Former MongoDB CEO Chirantan "CJ" Desai joins as chief enterprise platform officer, reporting directly to Zuckerberg.

Why it matters

It's Meta's attempt to turn its AI spending, on pace for up to $145 billion this year, into a new revenue line beyond ads, selling AI tools to other businesses the way Microsoft and Google already do.

The bear case

Meta has no track record selling enterprise software, and investors already knocked billions off Zuckerberg's fortune this week on doubts the AI spending will pay off.

What to watch

Which large customers Meta Enterprise Platform signs, and whether it shows up as a separate revenue line.

Mark ZuckerbergIPO / MarketsAI & techMETAAI read · ▼ Reality check

Zuckerberg loses $9 billion in a day on AI doubts

The move

Meta shares fell about 4% on Sept. 25, cutting Zuckerberg's Forbes-estimated net worth by $8.9 billion to $257.5 billion, after Goldman Sachs said AI hyperscalers need roughly $300 billion a year in AI-services revenue just to break even on their infrastructure spending, and about $1 trillion in AI-application spending for solid returns.

Why it matters

It was the single biggest one-day billionaire loss on the Forbes list that day and dropped Zuckerberg from 4th to 6th on the real-time rankings, a sign Wall Street is starting to question whether AI capex will pay for itself.

The bear case

Meta shares had surged roughly 36% in September on the strength of its new Muse AI assistant; a one-day pullback after a big run isn't proof investors are turning on the AI trade.

What to watch

Meta's next AI-spending commentary, and whether Goldman's hyperscaler break-even math becomes the market's general yardstick for AI stocks.

Meta stock jumps 25% since Zuckerberg unveiled Muse AI

The move

At Meta Connect on Sept. 24, Mark Zuckerberg unveiled the Muse Charm, a keychain-sized pendant with a fingerprint sensor that lets people talk to Meta's AI agent without opening a phone app, due to ship in December with no price given yet. He also said Meta will offer more than 100 styles of AI glasses by the end of 2026, including a $1,299 pair launching next spring and $349 camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses. Meta shares rose 4.5% that Thursday and are up more than 25% since Meta's Muse AI agent launched earlier in September.

Why it matters

Zuckerberg is racing to put an AI assistant into a physical device before OpenAI does, betting that hardware, not just apps, will decide who wins the AI-assistant race.

The bear case

Meta gave no price or firm ship date for the Muse Charm beyond "December," and its smart glasses line has already drawn privacy criticism over hidden-camera recording.

What to watch

The Muse Charm's December launch, its price, and whether privacy concerns slow smart-glasses sales.

Mark ZuckerbergProduct launchAI & techMETAAI read · ▲ Bullish

Meta stock jumps 11% in a day on Muse AI buzz

The move

Meta shares rose 11% in a single day and closed near a 52-week high after its Muse AI assistant, launched September 8, topped app-store charts with more than 2.5 million downloads. The move added over $100 billion to Meta's market cap, pushing its value close to $1.9 trillion.

Why it matters

Muse becoming a hit app is the market's first real evidence that Meta's massive AI spending can show up in a product people actually use, not just in ad-targeting algorithms.

The bear case

Most Muse features are still free, and it's unproven whether app-store buzz and downloads convert into paying subscribers at Meta's $20 or $100 monthly tiers.

What to watch

Whether Muse subscription uptake shows up in Meta's next earnings report.

Mark ZuckerbergProduct launchAI & techMETAAI read · ▲ Bullish · new revenue model

Zuckerberg turns Meta's AI agent into a toll booth

The move

At Meta Connect's Sept. 23-24 sessions, Zuckerberg said Muse, Meta's AI agent, will stay free for a large number of tokens, then earn Meta a small fee on transactions it completes. Meta added PayPal support and shopping integrations with Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Wayfair and American Eagle, plus travel booking through Expedia and grocery orders via Instacart.

Why it matters

Meta is betting that owning the AI agent people shop through is worth more than ad revenue alone. The fee comes from merchants, not users, so wider Muse adoption compounds directly into Meta's revenue.

The bear case

The model depends on people trusting an AI agent to spend money on their behalf, and Meta hasn't said what the fee will be or when it starts.

What to watch

How many retailers and users actually adopt Muse checkout once it leaves the keynote stage.
