Griffin says credit-card rate caps would hurt risky borrowers

The move

In comments published Oct. 7, Citadel founder Ken Griffin argued against a proposed 10% cap on credit card interest rates, saying lenders that can't price for risk would stop lending to borrowers with weak credit scores altogether. Two bills that would extend a cap — one from Sanders and Hawley, another from Ocasio-Cortez and Luna — had not cleared committee as of the article. The Federal Reserve put the average interest rate on card accounts charged interest at 22.15% in May 2026, and the CFPB found the 10 largest card issuers hold more than 80% of outstanding balances.

Why it matters

The fight touches more than $1.2 trillion in U.S. card debt and could reshape credit access for lower-income borrowers if a cap becomes law.

The bear case

Supporters of a cap point to cases like Illinois' 36% rate ceiling, meant to protect consumers from rates that can trap them in long-term debt.

What to watch

Whether either congressional bill advances out of committee.

Griffin gives $3 billion to Carnegie Mellon, builds Miami campus

The move

Ken Griffin donated $3 billion to Carnegie Mellon University, which the school is calling the largest individual gift in the history of higher education. Of that, $2 billion will fund a new Carnegie Mellon campus in Miami. CMU's School of Computer Science will be renamed for Griffin, who is joining the university's board of trustees.

Why it matters

The gift extends Griffin's bet on Miami as a hub for finance and tech talent, following Citadel's own relocation there and his separate $1.1 billion purchase of Wynwood land in September.

The bear case

Naming-rights megagifts draw criticism that university donations should prioritize need-based student aid over branding and campus expansion.

What to watch

Groundbreaking timeline for the Carnegie Mellon Miami campus.

Ken GriffinOtherFinancePrivateAI read · ▲ Biggest ever

Ken Griffin gives $3 billion to Carnegie Mellon, a record gift

The move

Citadel founder Ken Griffin pledged $3 billion to Carnegie Mellon University on Sept. 30, the largest individual gift in U.S. higher-education history, surpassing Phil and Penny Knight's $2 billion gift to Oregon Health & Science University in 2025. About $2 billion will fund a new Carnegie Mellon campus on a 35-acre site in Miami's Wynwood neighborhood, with construction starting in 2027 and students arriving in 2028. The remaining $1 billion goes to Carnegie Mellon's Pittsburgh campus, including $500 million for its School of Computer Science, which will be renamed the Kenneth C. Griffin School of Computer Science.

Why it matters

Griffin moved Citadel's headquarters from Chicago to Miami in 2022 and has become one of the city's biggest civic donors since; this gift ties a major research university's brand to the city he's betting on.

The bear case

A $2 billion campus that won't open until 2028 is a long-term bet that Miami's appeal to researchers and students holds up over several years, not a done deal.

What to watch

Whether other finance executives who moved to Miami make similarly large civic or academic donations there.
