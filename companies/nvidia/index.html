Nvidia and Apple each gave $25 million to Trump's ballroom

The move

A Washington Post report, based on an internal fundraising spreadsheet, named Nvidia and Apple as the largest donors to the White House ballroom project at $25 million each. The bear case

Nvidia's China AI chip share falls to 34%

The move

Nvidia shipped 678,000 AI data-center chips into China in the six months through June, down 44% from a year earlier, cutting its share of China's AI accelerator market to 34%, according to an IDC report cited by Reuters. Huawei's share rose to 28%, with smaller Chinese chipmakers T-Head, Cambricon, Kunlunxin and Hygon splitting the rest.

Why it matters

U.S. export limits on Nvidia's most advanced chips, plus Beijing's push toward domestic alternatives, are opening real room for Huawei in a market Nvidia once dominated almost completely.

The bear case

Nvidia still leads the global AI chip market by a wide margin, and China is a shrinking slice of its total sales as demand elsewhere keeps growing faster.

What to watch

Whether Washington eases or tightens chip export rules in the coming months.

Amazon explores $8 billion investor financing for Nvidia chips

The move

Amazon is in talks with outside investors over roughly $8 billion of financing built around its Nvidia chips, the Financial Times reported October 2. Under the plan, the chips would move into a special-purpose vehicle that raises money from investors, with Amazon leasing them back for its data centers. As of the report, no deal had been finalized and Amazon had not commented.

Why it matters

The structure would let Amazon keep using the chips in AWS while shifting some of the capital cost off its own balance sheet, as its 2026 capex is expected to run around $220 billion.

The bear case

Advanced AI chips depreciate fast — Bloomberg has flagged roughly a five-year replacement cycle — so outside investors in the vehicle would bear the risk that newer Nvidia generations erode the hardware's value before the financing is paid off.

What to watch

Whether Amazon and its investors finalize the structure, and whether other hyperscalers adopt similar off-balance-sheet chip financing.

Nvidia adds $150B buyback, the biggest ever

The move

Nvidia's board authorized an additional $150 billion for share buybacks on Sept. 28, lifting its total remaining authorization to $235 billion through fiscal 2028. The company called it the largest single buyback-authorization increase in corporate history, topping Apple's $110 billion authorization from 2024; the stock closed up 1.68% at $228.86 that day. Shares then hit a record high on Oct. 2, gaining 2.9% to push Nvidia's market value to roughly $5.7 trillion — less than $300 billion away from becoming the first public company to reach $6 trillion, and a rebound of nearly 25% from its late-July low after a two-month selloff had erased more than $1 trillion in value.

Why it matters

A buyback this size signals Nvidia's board sees enough free cash flow to fund both its AI data-center build-out and shareholder returns at the same time, at a moment when rivals like Oracle are piling on debt to do the former alone.

The bear case

A giant buyback can also be read as a company running out of better uses for its cash, and Nvidia's valuation now leaves very little room for a slowdown in AI chip demand to go unpunished.

What to watch

Whether Nvidia actually becomes the first company to close above a $6 trillion market cap.

Mark ZuckerbergPolicyAI & techMETA · TSLA · NVDAAI read · — Neutral

Zuckerberg, Musk, Huang sign voluntary AI safety accord

The move

Six AI and chip company leaders — Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Google's Sundar Pichai, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, OpenAI's Greg Brockman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Tesla/SpaceX/xAI's Elon Musk — signed a one-page White House 'Accord on Super Intelligence' on Sept. 29. It commits signers to four layers of safety controls for frontier AI models: internal technical monitoring, an internal oversight team, an independent external auditor and an independent board committee. The accord is not legally binding and sets no penalties for falling short; it grew out of talks between Zuckerberg and House Speaker Mike Johnson, with Huang helping draft the principles beforehand.

Why it matters

It's a rare moment of several rival AI leaders agreeing to the same safety framework, even informally, while governments are still deciding how much to regulate AI.

The bear case

With no enforcement mechanism, public disclosure requirement or penalty for noncompliance, the accord is a voluntary statement of intent rather than a binding commitment.

What to watch

Whether any signer publishes audit results, or whether Congress tries to turn the accord into binding law.
