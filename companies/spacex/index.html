Goldman raises SpaceX price target to $230 before earnings

The move

Goldman Sachs raised its price target on SpaceX to $230 from $220 in an Oct. 7 note, keeping a Buy rating and implying about 45% upside from a $158.96 reference price. Goldman raised its SpaceX sales forecasts to roughly $51.6 billion for 2026, $127.5 billion for 2027 and $205.6 billion for 2028, and estimated AI-related revenue could reach $27.3 billion this year, rising to $146.7 billion by 2028. The note also cited Sensor Tower data showing Starlink's monthly active users up 21% year over year.

Why it matters

The upgrade reflects a Wall Street view that SpaceX's Starlink and AI-compute ambitions, not just rockets, are what will drive its valuation going forward.

The bear case

By the article's own math, SpaceX already trades at roughly 122 times this year's expected earnings at that reference price, a multiple that assumes years of fast, uninterrupted growth.

What to watch

SpaceX's upcoming earnings report, which Goldman's note was published ahead of.

Musk becomes a trillionaire again as SpaceX surges

The move

Elon Musk's net worth hit about $1 trillion on Oct. 5, up $30.6 billion from the prior session, as SpaceX shares rose more than 5% to around $167 and Tesla shares also gained after a strong delivery quarter. Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas kept an overweight rating on SpaceX with a $300 price target. Musk also confirmed early talks with TSMC about a dedicated chip plant, Terafab, to supply Tesla, SpaceX and xAI. Musk briefly passed $1 trillion in June before giving it back as both stocks slid.

Why it matters

It's Musk's second trip over $1 trillion this year, and this time the move is tied to a strong Tesla delivery quarter, a clean Starship orbital launch and bullish analyst calls rather than hype alone.

The bear case

Both stocks are concentrated bets on Musk's own projects; if Starship or Tesla deliveries disappoint next quarter, the trillion-dollar mark could evaporate again as it did in June.

What to watch

Whether the SpaceX-TSMC Terafab talks turn into a signed deal.

SpaceX puts Starship into orbit for the first time

The move

SpaceX launched Starship's Flight 14 from Starbase, Texas, on Sept. 28. The rocket reached orbit for the first time and released 26 Starlink V3 satellites, then splashed down in the Northern Pacific after one of its six Raptor vacuum engines shut down early during ascent.

Why it matters

Reaching orbit is the milestone NASA's Artemis moon-landing plan and Starlink's global buildout both depend on, and it keeps SpaceX far ahead of any rival heavy-lift rocket.

The bear case

An engine cut out mid-ascent and the flight team cut the mission short as a precaution — Starship still has real reliability problems to work out before it can fly astronauts.

What to watch

The next Starship test flight and progress on the NASA-contracted human landing system.

SpaceX clears FAA hurdle for Starship's biggest test yet

The move

The FAA gave SpaceX final approval on Sept. 26 for Starship Flight 14, an orbital test that will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites for the first time, using Ship 41 and Super Heavy Booster 21. The flight is set for Monday, Sept. 28, after a wet dress rehearsal and a delay tied to public-safety concerns.

Why it matters

A clean flight would prove Starship V3 can carry a full Starlink payload and reenter intact, a key step in SpaceX's push to keep launching at a pace that supports its roughly $1.95 trillion valuation.

The bear case

SpaceX already scrubbed and rescheduled this exact flight once this month; another delay or a failed reentry would raise fresh doubts about V3's reliability.

What to watch

Whether Flight 14 launches Monday and whether both stages survive reentry.

SpaceX stacks Starship for its first attempt to reach orbit

The move

SpaceX stacked its Ship 41 upper stage atop Super Heavy booster B21 at Starbase, Texas on Sept. 23, forming a 124-meter rocket for Starship's Flight 14, the vehicle's first attempt to reach orbit. The mission would deploy 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites over about a 10-hour flight, far longer than the roughly one-hour suborbital hops of earlier Starship flights. SpaceX is targeting a launch window opening as early as 7:15 a.m. CDT on Sept. 28, pending FAA approval; a temporary flight restriction is in place through Oct. 7.

Why it matters

An actual orbital attempt, rather than another suborbital hop, is the milestone SpaceX needs to start using Starship for real missions like Starlink deployment and, eventually, crewed flights.

The bear case

Earlier Starship test flights have ended in explosions or lost vehicles, and the FAA has not yet granted final approval for a vehicle this large to fly an orbital profile.

What to watch

The Flight 14 launch window opening Sept. 28, and whether the FAA grants approval in time.
