Huang's fortune tops $200 billion as Nvidia hits record

The move

Nvidia shares hit an all-time high of $236.13 on Oct 2, after Morgan Stanley reinstated the stock as its top semiconductor pick. Forbes' real-time estimate put Jensen Huang's net worth at $204.5 billion that day, up $4.7 billion in a single session.

Why it matters

It's the clearest sign yet that Wall Street still trusts the AI buildout, even after a summer of valuation worries.

The bear case

A single-day stock pop isn't an earnings report. Nvidia's next real test is its quarterly results, not an analyst note.

What to watch

Nvidia's next quarterly earnings call

Nvidia adds a record $150 billion to its buyback

The move

Nvidia's board approved another $150 billion for share repurchases on Sept. 28, its largest single increase ever. Total remaining buyback capacity is now $235 billion, which the company expects to spend by fiscal 2028. Nvidia already bought back about $39 billion of stock in the first two quarters of this fiscal year.

Why it matters

It's a statement that Nvidia is generating more cash from the AI buildout than it can spend on R&D and capacity alone, and Jensen Huang wants shareholders to feel that.

The bear case

Buybacks this size can also be read as a company with no bigger acquisition or investment to make — a plateau signal dressed up as confidence.

What to watch

How much of the $235 billion Nvidia actually spends before its next earnings report.

Jensen HuangPolicyAI & techNVDA · BABAAI read · ▲ Cautious optimism

China signals it may let Alibaba, ByteDance buy new Nvidia chip

The move

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology has asked Alibaba and ByteDance about their planned orders for Nvidia's new RTX Pro 5500 workstation chip, and officials have signaled the purchases are likely to be approved, The Information reported Sept. 27. Nothing is finalized.

Why it matters

The RTX Pro 5500, an 84GB Blackwell-based card, was built to sit under U.S. export-control thresholds; a Chinese green light would reopen a major market Nvidia has largely lost to export curbs.

The bear case

This is a signal relayed by unnamed officials, not confirmed policy, and Beijing has reversed similar chip-buying openings before.

What to watch

Formal confirmation from MIIT and whether Alibaba or ByteDance actually place orders.

Jensen HuangProduct launchAI & techNVDAAI read · ▲ Bullish

Huang says Nvidia could double chip sales next year

The move

Speaking in Scotland on September 19, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said the company could sell twice as many chips next year as this year, driven by demand for its new Vera Rubin architecture. Wall Street estimates put that near $700 billion in fiscal 2028 revenue, up about 70% from an expected $411 billion in fiscal 2027.

Why it matters

Huang says every frontier AI company plans to adopt Vera Rubin at launch, unlike the prior Blackwell Ultra generation, which is why he's more confident about next year's growth.

The bear case

Doubling chip sales assumes AI infrastructure spending keeps accelerating at its current pace, with no pullback from hyperscaler customers.

What to watch

Nvidia's next earnings call for updated Vera Rubin order and shipment numbers.

Sep 24, 2026 edition
Jensen HuangPolicyAI & techNVDAAI read · — Binary event, no resolution

Trump and Xi meet, but Nvidia's China question stays open

The move

Trump hosted Xi Jinping at the White House on Sept. 24. AI safety cooperation came up in the talks, but chip export controls remained a sticking point and neither side signaled it would slow the AI race.

Why it matters

Nvidia's own guidance already assumes zero data-center chip sales to China, so any real opening would be pure upside. The summit didn't deliver one.

The bear case

Even where Washington has approved chip export licenses for Chinese firms, Beijing has reportedly discouraged its own companies from taking delivery, so a policy win in Washington may not translate into actual sales.

What to watch

Any follow-up announcement on chip licensing after this week's talks.
