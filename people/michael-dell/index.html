Dell falls as CoreWeave's bond rout spreads AI-debt fears

The move

Dell shares fell roughly 11% on October 14 as CoreWeave's bonds traded like distressed debt, with yields near 15%, deepening investor worry about how AI-infrastructure companies are financing their buildouts. Oracle and Nebius also fell that day.

Why it matters

Dell supplies servers for AI data centers, so investors read CoreWeave's financing strain as a warning sign for the AI hardware supply chain, Dell included.

The bear case

CoreWeave's CEO called the bond-market reaction irrational, saying its contracted backlog covers its debt obligations, and Dell's own hardware demand hasn't shown signs of slowing.

What to watch

Whether CoreWeave's bond yields keep climbing and change how AI infrastructure deals get financed.

Dell drops 11% on Barclays downgrade over AI doubts

The move

Dell Technologies shares fell 11.3% on Oct 14, 2026, far more than the S&P 500's 1.1% decline, after Barclays cut the stock to Equal Weight from Overweight. Barclays said it believes Dell's strong enterprise revenue reflects elevated memory-chip costs more than a real shift toward enterprise AI spending, and made similar downgrades to HPE and NetApp the same day.

Why it matters

Dell's stock had run up on hopes that businesses are buying AI-ready servers in volume; Barclays' note challenges that story and questions how much of the recent growth is actually AI-driven.

The bear case

Dell's revenue still grew, and one analyst's reframing of the cause doesn't change the order backlog the company has already booked.

What to watch

Dell's next earnings report for concrete data on enterprise AI order volume.

Trump awards top science medals to six tech leaders

The move

At an October 8 "Science: A New Golden Age" summit, President Trump gave the National Medal of Science to Elon Musk, Sergey Brin, Jensen Huang and Lisa Su, and the National Medal of Technology and Innovation to Satya Nadella and Michael Dell. Trump gives Musk, Brin, Huang science medals at White House

The move

President Trump awarded the National Medal of Science to Elon Musk, Sergey Brin, Jensen Huang and AMD's Lisa Su at a Washington ceremony on Thursday, October 8. Michael Dell and Microsoft's Satya Nadella received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation instead. Trump called Musk "our modern-day Thomas Edison" and said, "Because of these people here, we will always be on top. No one else can compete with us." Hours before the ceremony, the administration suspended Microsoft and several other companies from the green-card sponsorship program.

Why it matters

The honors double as a message that the administration wants to keep its biggest AI and chip players close, even as it tightens the visa pipeline many of the same companies rely on for talent.

The bear case

A medal ceremony changes nothing about earnings, chip supply or visa policy; it's a photo opportunity timed to distract from the visa suspension announced the same morning.

What to watch

Whether the Permanent Labor Certification suspension affecting Microsoft and Adobe expands to other sponsors.

Michael DellPolicyOtherPrivateAI read · — Big philanthropic bet

Michael Dell defends letting donors gift stock to kids' accounts

The move

In an interview published Oct. 8, Michael Dell defended a rule change that lets donors fund children's federal Trump Accounts with individual company stocks rather than only low-cost index funds. Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion to put $250 into the accounts of 25 million children; more than 10 million children have received the money so far, totaling $2.6 billion, with the rest expected by Friday. As of Oct. 1, the Treasury Department moved to automatic enrollment, and nearly 70 million children under 18 with a valid Social Security number now have an account, though parents or guardians must claim them.

Why it matters

It's one of the largest personal commitments tied to the new federal children's savings program, which just moved to automatic enrollment for tens of millions of kids.

The bear case

Critics say letting donors gift single stocks, instead of index funds, ties children's savings to the fortunes of one company and could let wealthy donors influence the price of the stocks they give.

What to watch

Whether the Dells' full $6.25 billion pledge is completed by the stated Friday deadline.

Michael DellIPO / MarketsAI & techDELLAI read · ▲ Bullish

Dell stock jumps on record $95B AI server backlog

The move

Dell shares rose about 4% on Oct. 2 after the company reported a record AI server order backlog of $95 billion. Dell had already raised its full-year revenue forecast on Sept. 1 to a midpoint of $192 billion, citing AI server demand, with management saying the main bottleneck is memory chip supply — DRAM and NAND. Rival Hewlett Packard Enterprise also jumped 8% the same day after a large AI systems order from cloud provider Vultr.

Why it matters

A $95 billion backlog shows AI infrastructure demand is still outrunning supply, which is good for Dell's hardware business even as chip shortages cap how fast it can ship.

The bear case

A backlog is a promise to ship, not booked revenue; memory shortages could delay deliveries and push some of that revenue into later years.

What to watch

Whether Dell converts the backlog into shipped revenue without further guidance cuts tied to component shortages.

Michael DellInvestmentAI & techDELLAI read · ▲ Riding Japan's AI buildout

Dell, JERA sign deal for $15 billion Tokyo AI data center

The move

Dell Technologies, Japanese power producer JERA and UK infrastructure firm RHAELM signed a memorandum of understanding on Oct 1 for a 400-megawatt AI data center next to JERA's Chiba Thermal Power Station, costing more than $15 billion. Apollo Global Management is a financing partner, and the companies said a wider rollout across Japan could eventually reach $140 billion.

Why it matters

It's a direct bet that AI data centers can be built faster by sitting next to existing power plants instead of waiting on grid connections.

The bear case

This is a non-binding memorandum, not a signed construction contract, and the $140 billion figure for future sites is unconfirmed.

What to watch

A binding agreement and groundbreaking, targeted for operations around 2028
