Reliance's Jio Platforms opens India's largest-ever IPO

The move

Jio Platforms opened its initial public offering to the public on Oct. 21, with a price band of 1,065 to 1,119 rupees per share. At the top of that band the fresh-issue offering would raise about 30,213 crore rupees (roughly $3.6 billion), which Indian financial press describe as the country's largest-ever IPO by issue size. Subscription closes Oct. 23. Reliance Industries holds more than 66% of Jio Platforms, with a Meta affiliate near 10% and Google holding about 7.7%, according to LSEG data.

Why it matters

A successful listing would give Ambani's Reliance a large new source of capital and a public market valuation for its telecom and digital arm.

The bear case

The final issue price and allotment results won't be known until subscription closes, and Reliance retains firm control regardless of how the public portion prices.

What to watch

Jio Platforms' subscription numbers when the window closes Oct. 23, and its stock-market debut.

Reliance pays staff early ahead of record Jio IPO

The move

Ahead of Jio Platforms' IPO, Reliance paid October salaries to its nearly 420,000 group employees about 15 days early and encouraged them to invest, without saying why, according to a Bloomberg report. The IPO's anchor book opens Oct. 19 and public bidding runs Oct. 21-23, targeting up to 30,200 crore rupees (about $3.2 billion) at a price band of 1,065 to 1,119 rupees a share, valuing Jio at up to 10.3 lakh crore rupees (about $108 billion). Up to 500 crore rupees of shares are set aside for eligible Jio employees at a 5% discount.

Why it matters

It's one of India's largest-ever IPOs, and the early pay and outreach show how hard Reliance is pushing to build demand beyond big institutional investors.

The bear case

Encouraging employees to put personal savings into a company-linked stock concentrates their financial risk in one bet.

What to watch

Jio Platforms' public subscription window, Oct. 21-23.

Musk accuses 'oligarchs,' asks if Ambani is India's real boss

The move

On October 7, Musk said on X that unnamed "oligarchs" were blocking Starlink's launch in India to protect their own businesses. The next day he asked whether Mukesh Ambani was the "real boss of India." Starlink received an Indian license in July 2025 but still hasn't launched commercial service there; India's government has rejected Musk's claims.

The bear case

Publicly feuding with India's government and its most powerful businessman is an unusual way to get a stalled license approved, and risks delaying the launch further.

What to watch

Whether Starlink receives final operational clearance to launch commercial service in India.

Musk escalates Starlink attack on Ambani in India

The move

On October 9, Elon Musk posted on X calling Mukesh Ambani India's real "boss" and accusing him of "monopolistic exploitation" for blocking Starlink's launch in the country. India's telecom minister, Jyotiraditya Scindia, said the country "does not allow a monopoly in any sector." India is one of the biggest untapped markets for satellite internet, and the dispute shows how much friction remains before Starlink can compete there against Ambani's Reliance Jio.

The bear case

Musk's public attacks on a named rival look like pressure tactics rather than a regulatory complaint, and India's government has already rejected the monopoly claim.

What to watch

Whether Starlink receives final security clearance to launch commercial service in India.

Reliance lines up second $1 billion rupee bond sale in weeks

The move

Reliance Industries is planning to raise 100 billion rupees (about $1.04 billion) through 10-year rupee bonds at a 7.90% coupon, with bids expected within one to two weeks, Bloomberg reported Sept. 24. It follows an earlier sale of 120 billion rupees (about $1.25 billion) of 5-year notes at 7.47% roughly two weeks earlier. Together the two sales add up to about $2.3 billion in new rupee debt in one month, taking Reliance's outstanding bonds to 540 billion rupees.

Why it matters

Reliance is racing to lock in local borrowing costs before the Reserve Bank of India's Oct. 7 policy decision, at a time when rupee funding has gotten cheaper than dollar debt as U.S. Treasury yields rise.

The bear case

Taking on $2.3 billion in new debt in a single month adds to Reliance's liabilities regardless of how favorable today's rates look, and the bet only pays off if the RBI doesn't cut rates further than expected.

What to watch

Whether Reliance's bond sale prices before the Reserve Bank of India's Oct. 7 policy decision.
