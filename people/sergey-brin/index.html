Google loses bid to limit $3.2B ad-tech damages claim

The move

A federal judge in New York, Kevin Castel, ruled on Sept. 30 that a class of roughly 5,000 publishers can pursue about $1.7 billion in damages from Google over its AdX ad exchange, with USA Today separately able to seek about $1 billion (before trebling) and Daily Mail General and Trust about $600 million. The judge rejected Google's challenge to the damage calculations and dismissed claims from publishers using different ad-buying tools. The case builds on a 2023 Justice Department lawsuit in which a Virginia judge already found Google illegally monopolized ad-tech markets.

Why it matters

This moves Google's ad-tech liability from a legal theory into dollar figures a jury could actually award, and antitrust lawyers say the ruling increases pressure on Google to settle rather than go to trial.

The bear case

Under U.S. antitrust law, damages can be tripled, so the publishers' claims could realistically be worth far more than the headline $3.2 billion if a jury sides with them.

What to watch

Whether Google moves toward a settlement with the publisher class before a trial date is set.
