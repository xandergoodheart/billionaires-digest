How markets work

Coins are play money. They have no value. You can't buy, sell, cash out or send them to anyone. Everyone starts with 1,000 coins and can claim 250 more each week while under 5,000.

Shares pay 1 coin if they are right. Buy YES if you think the answer will be yes, NO if not. When the market is settled, each winning share pays 1 coin and losing shares pay nothing. If it is void (for example a tie), everyone gets back the coins they put in.

The price is a running guess. A YES price of 62% means one more YES share costs about 0.62 coins right now. Every buy nudges the price toward the side bought, and bigger buys move it more. An automatic market maker sets the price (it uses a formula called the logarithmic market scoring rule), so there is always a price and you never wait for someone to take the other side. Buys can't push a price past 99%.

You can sell before it closes. Selling gives shares back to the market at the current price, rounded down to a whole coin. Trading stops Friday at 4:00 PM New York time. You can spend up to 500 coins per trade.

Results come from public data. Head-to-head and sector questions use the fantasy league's scores for the week. Insider-buy questions use Form 4 filings on SEC EDGAR filed Monday to Friday. Each result links to its source. Nothing here is investment advice.
